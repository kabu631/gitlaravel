=== nothing-phone-3
<p>The Nothing Phone (3) is the brand's first attempt at a flagship. It has wireless charging, three 50MP cameras with a periscope, IP68 and a tiny round display on its back called the Glyph Matrix. It is a phone people either want at once or never.</p>
<h3>Design</h3>
<p>The transparent back arranges the cameras unevenly on purpose and adds a 25mm dot-matrix screen in the corner. It shows the time, timers, the charging level, who is calling, and small toys such as a spin-the-bottle. It replaces the light strips of earlier models, and some fans miss them.</p>
<h3>Display and performance</h3>
<p>A 6.67-inch AMOLED at 120Hz with even borders is bright and pleasant. The Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 with 12 or 16GB of memory is quick, one step below true flagship chips. At Rs 1,09,999 a <a href="/products/poco-f8-pro">POCO F8 Pro</a> or <a href="/products/oneplus-15r">OnePlus 15R</a> is faster.</p>
<h3>Cameras</h3>
<p>A 50MP main sensor, a 50MP ultra wide and a 50MP 3x periscope that focuses close, with a 50MP front camera. Processing is natural, sometimes a little dark. For portraits and street pictures it is enjoyable, with good film-like presets. Video is 4K at 60 frames on all lenses.</p>
<h3>Battery</h3>
<p>5,150mAh in the global model, with 65W wired and 15W wireless charging. A day is normal and two is not. A good PPS charger works, as Nothing uses open standards.</p>
<h3>Nothing OS</h3>
<p>Clean, monochrome and consistent, with five Android versions promised and longer security support. The Essential Key saves screenshots and voice notes and tries to organise them.</p>
<h3>Price in Nepal</h3>

<h3>Phone 3 or Phone 4a?</h3>

<h3>Who it is for</h3>
<p>People who are tired of identical slabs and value software that leaves them alone. If you count specifications, others give more. Service is in Kathmandu, so ask about parts before buying.</p>
