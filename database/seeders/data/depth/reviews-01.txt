<p><em>How we rated it:</em> We have not run our own lab test of the Galaxy S24. This review is based on Samsung's specifications, what is known about the phone after almost three years on sale, and how it compares with phones you can buy in Nepal today.</p>
<p>The Galaxy S24 came out in January 2024 as the small, sensible member of Samsung's flagship family. It is no longer sold new through official channels in Nepal, so most people reading this in late 2026 are looking at a used or leftover unit and asking one question: is it still worth having, or should the money go to a new mid-range phone? The short answer is that it has aged well, with one clear weakness that you need to plan around.</p>
<h2>Which S24 are you looking at?</h2>
<p>Before anything else, find out which chip is inside. Samsung sold the S24 with its own Exynos 2400 in most of the world and with the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 for Galaxy in the United States, South Korea and China. Units sold officially in Nepal belong to the Exynos group. Units brought in from America by relatives, or through grey-market shops, may have the Snapdragon.</p>
<p>In daily use the gap is smaller than forum arguments suggest. Messaging, photos, maps, YouTube and banking apps feel the same on both. The Snapdragon holds its speed longer in heavy games and uses a little less power while doing it. If you play Genshin Impact or PUBG for an hour at a time, it is worth paying slightly more for that version. If you do not, buy whichever unit is in better condition. You can check in Settings under About phone, where the model number ends in B for the international version and U or U1 for the American one.</p>
<h2>Size is the reason to buy it</h2>
<p>Very few good phones are this small any more. The S24 has a 6.2-inch screen and weighs 167 grams, which means you can reach most of the display with one thumb and forget it is in a shirt pocket. Compare that with the 6.8 and 6.9-inch phones that fill shop counters on New Road. If you ride a scooter, hold your phone on crowded buses, or simply have smaller hands, this matters every single day in a way that a faster chip does not.</p>
<p>The screen is also excellent. It is a Dynamic AMOLED panel that runs between 1Hz and 120Hz and reaches 2,600 nits at its peak, so maps stay readable in direct sun. Resolution is Full HD+, which at this size looks perfectly sharp.</p>
[[specs:samsung-galaxy-s24]]
<h2>Cameras: still better than most mid-rangers</h2>
<p>The camera set is a 50MP main sensor, a 12MP ultra wide and a 10MP telephoto with 3x optical zoom. That telephoto is the quiet advantage over a new phone at a similar used price. Mid-range phones usually replace the zoom lens with a 2MP macro camera that nobody uses. On the S24 you can photograph a child on a school stage, a temple detail or a distant peak without the picture turning to mush, as long as there is reasonable light.</p>
<p>Samsung's processing leans towards bright faces and vivid skies. Some people like that, others find it heavy. Night mode works well on the main camera and noticeably less well on the other two. Video goes up to 8K, but 4K at 60 frames per second is the setting worth using because files stay manageable and all the stabilisation works.</p>
<p>The selfie camera is 12MP with autofocus, which helps with group shots taken at arm's length.</p>
<h2>The battery is the catch</h2>
<p>There is no polite way around it. The S24 has a 4,000mAh battery in a year when budget phones carry 6,000mAh and some mid-rangers reach 10,000mAh. Modern screens and a slim body make the most of it, but if you are out from morning college until evening tuition with mobile data on, expect to look for a charger before bed. On 5G the drain is faster still.</p>
<p>Charging tops out at 25W by cable. It also supports wireless charging and can charge earbuds from its back, both rare in the price range where used units now sit. Samsung did not put a charger in the box, so check whether a seller is including one, and use a USB Power Delivery charger that supports PPS to get the full speed.</p>
<p>There is a second issue with any phone from early 2024: the battery inside has been through a few hundred charge cycles. Lithium cells lose capacity with every cycle. A used S24 with eighty-five per cent of its original capacity behaves like a 3,400mAh phone. Include the price of a battery replacement at a Samsung service centre in your budget, and treat a unit that already has a new genuine battery as worth more.</p>
<h2>Software support is its best long-term feature</h2>
<p>Samsung promised seven years of Android upgrades and security patches for the S24 family, which takes it to 2031. Almost no mid-range phone on sale in Nepal today can match that. It launched with Android 14 and One UI 6.1 and has been updated since. For someone who keeps a phone until it breaks, that promise counts for more than the processor.</p>
<p>The Galaxy AI tools that were the headline in 2024 still work: Circle to Search for identifying something on the screen, call and text translation, and generative photo edits. Language support varies, and Nepali is not covered by every feature, so do not buy the phone for translation without trying it first.</p>
<h2>What to check on a used unit</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>MDMS registration.</strong> Dial *#06#, note the IMEI and check it on the NTA portal before you hand over money. An unregistered phone can lose network access.</li>
<li><strong>Screen.</strong> Open a plain white and a plain grey image at low brightness. Look for green tint, faint keyboard outlines or dark patches along the edges.</li>
<li><strong>Accounts.</strong> The previous owner must remove their Google and Samsung accounts in front of you, otherwise the phone locks after a reset.</li>
<li><strong>Water seals.</strong> The S24 is rated IP68 from the factory. A phone that has been opened for repair no longer has that protection.</li>
<li><strong>Knox.</strong> In the service menu or in Settings, confirm that Knox has not been tripped. If it has, banking features and Samsung Wallet may be blocked.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What else the money buys</h2>
<p>A new phone such as the <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a57-5g">Galaxy A57 5G</a> gives you a fresh battery, a full warranty and a bigger screen, but loses the telephoto lens, the compact size and the wireless charging. A <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s25">Galaxy S25</a>, where you can find one, keeps the size and adds a faster chip. If you want the newest small Samsung, look at the <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s26">Galaxy S26</a>, and expect to pay properly for it.</p>
<p>Our rule of thumb: an S24 in clean condition, with registration and a healthy battery, is a better buy than a new phone at the same price for anyone who cares about size, zoom and long updates. It is a worse buy for gamers and for people who are away from a socket all day.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>The Galaxy S24 was never the most exciting phone of its year, and that has helped it. It does nearly everything well, it fits in a hand, and it will still be receiving updates when many newer phones have been forgotten. Buy it for the size, the zoom lens and the support. Keep a power bank in your bag and you will get along fine.</p>

<h2>The screen, and why the coating matters more than the nits</h2>

<h2>Discs or downloads: do the sums first</h2>
<p>Physical games matter more in Nepal than in many countries. Internet packages can make a 100GB download slow, and discs can be swapped with friends or sold when you finish. If you buy six games a year and sell each one for half of what you paid, a disc drive can save you tens of thousands of rupees over the life of the console.</p>
<p>On the other side, digital stores run sales all year, and subscription plans include a large library. With a stable fibre connection and a habit of waiting for discounts, you may never miss the drive. There is a trap, though: a digital game is tied to your account for ever and has no resale value.</p>
<p>If you are unsure, remember that Sony sells a detachable drive for both the Digital Edition and the Pro. Check that it is available and what it costs before you count on adding it later. At the moment availability here is irregular.</p>
<h2>What the Pro improves</h2>
<p>The Pro's larger graphics section, at 16.7 TFLOPS compared with about 10.3, does two things. It lets games run at higher resolution while keeping 60 frames per second, and it uses Sony's AI upscaling to sharpen the image. In games with a Pro patch you often get the quality mode's visuals at the performance mode's speed.</p>
<p>Whether you can see that depends on your television. On a 4K set of 55 inches or more, viewed from two or three metres, the difference is visible in fine detail and steadier motion. On a 1080p TV or a 32-inch screen, most people will struggle to tell. If your TV is not 4K, put the money towards a better one before you think about the Pro.</p>
<h2>TV features that matter</h2>
<p>Look for HDMI 2.1, a 120Hz panel, variable refresh rate and a low input lag game mode. A basic 4K 60Hz TV still looks good with a PS5 and is all that many games need. Use the HDMI cable from the box, as it is certified for the full bandwidth, and turn on the TV's game mode and its enhanced HDMI setting. More disappointing first impressions come from a wrong TV setting than from the console.</p>
<h2>Storage: plan for it on day one</h2>
<p>System files take up a part of the drive, so the Digital Edition's 825GB leaves much less for games. Recent blockbusters take between 80GB and 200GB each. Every PS5 has a slot for an M.2 NVMe drive. Buy a PCIe Gen4 model with a read speed of at least 5,500MB/s and a heatsink that fits the slot. Installing it takes a screwdriver and ten minutes, and Sony's support page shows each step.</p>
<p>An ordinary USB hard drive can store PS4 games and play them, but PS5 games can only be kept there as an archive and must be copied back before playing. It is a cheap way to avoid downloading a game twice.</p>
<h2>GTA VI and the reason for the rush</h2>
<p>The launch of GTA VI sent many buyers to the shops at once, and prices moved with demand. If that game is your only reason, any of the three consoles will run it. Wait a few weeks if you can. Launch-window stock is expensive, bundles appear later, and the first patches usually improve performance. Our <a href="/news/gta-6-launch-record-sales-how-to-buy-nepal">GTA VI buying article</a> covers editions and regional accounts.</p>
<h2>Accounts, regions and paying for games</h2>
<p>There is no Nepal PlayStation Store, so most people use an account from another country. Choose the region carefully at the start, because it cannot be changed, and downloadable content must match the region of the game. Gift cards must match the account region as well. Buy them only from sellers you trust, as reversed or stolen codes can get an account banned. Games on disc work on any console, but extra content for them follows the region of the disc.</p>
<p>Online play in most paid games needs a PlayStation Plus subscription. Free-to-play games such as Fortnite do not. Work out the yearly cost when you compare consoles with a gaming laptop.</p>
<h2>Power, heat and safety</h2>
<p>Use a proper surge protector, and better still a UPS that lets you shut down safely. A power cut in the middle of a system update or a save can damage data. Put the console in rest mode only when your power is reliable; otherwise shut it down fully. Leave at least ten centimetres of space around it, do not enclose it in a glass cabinet, and clean the dust ports with a soft brush and a vacuum cleaner every few months. Kathmandu dust is the most common cause of noisy fans.</p>
<h2>Which one to buy</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Digital Edition:</strong> the right choice for a tight budget, a good internet connection and people who mostly play one or two big games. Add an SSD later.</li>
<li><strong>Slim Disc:</strong> only if you already own PS4 or PS5 discs, or will buy and sell them regularly. At today's listing its price is too close to the Pro.</li>
<li><strong>Pro:</strong> the best hardware, and at a gap of about Rs 5,000 over the Slim Disc it is strangely good value. Make sure your TV deserves it.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Buying used</h2>
<p>A second-hand PS5 can be a good deal. Ask to see it running a demanding game for twenty minutes and listen for loud fans or coil whine. Check that the disc drive reads both a game and a Blu-ray if it has one, that the controller sticks do not drift, and that the console is not banned: sign in with your own account and go online. Look at the HDMI port for bent pins, since that repair is common and not cheap. Original boxes and bills help if you want to sell it on later.</p>
