=== airpods-pro-3-review-nepal
<h2>Specifications</h2>
[[specs:airpods-pro-3]]
<h2>The case for them in one week of use</h2>
<p>Feature lists do not explain why iPhone owners stop looking at other earbuds. A normal week does. On Monday you open the case beside a phone and they connect. On Tuesday a call arrives on your Mac during a video on your iPad, and the sound moves by itself. On Wednesday you take one bud out to talk to a shopkeeper and the music pauses. On Thursday you leave them in a teashop, and Find My shows the case on a map and lets you play a sound. None of this needs an app or a setting. Other brands match individual pieces. Nobody else gets all of it on an iPhone, since Apple does not give outside companies the same access.</p>
<h2>Noise cancelling and Transparency</h2>
<p>These are among the quietest earbuds you can buy. The new foam-infused tips matter as much as the electronics, because they block more sound physically. Apple includes a wider range of sizes than before. Run the fit test in Settings with each of two sizes, and choose the one that stays put when you chew.</p>
<p>Transparency is the mode that other makers still chase. Voices sound natural, as if you were not wearing anything, and sudden loud sounds such as a horn are softened. For walking in city traffic it is the safer choice than full cancellation. Adaptive mode mixes the two and changes as you move, which works well in an office and is less predictable on a street.</p>
<h2>Sound</h2>
<p>The balance is lively with stronger bass than the previous model and clear vocals. Spatial audio with head tracking is impressive with films on an iPad and a matter of taste with music. There is no high-resolution codec: iPhones send AAC over Bluetooth. At a decent bitrate AAC sounds very good, and few people can identify a difference in a blind test on the move. If you collect lossless music and listen in silence, wired headphones remain the better answer.</p>
<h2>Health features</h2>
<p>Apple has added sensors and hearing tools to this line, including a hearing test and an assistance mode in supported countries, along with heart-rate sensing during workouts. Availability of the medical functions depends on your region, and Nepal is often absent from those lists. Do not buy them as a hearing aid. The heart-rate reading is useful for people who train with an iPhone and do not own an Apple Watch.</p>
<h2>Fit, sweat and rain</h2>
<p>Each bud weighs about 5.5 grams, and the angle of the stem has been adjusted so that most people forget them after ten minutes. IP57 for buds and case is a real improvement. It covers dust and a short dip in fresh water, so running in drizzle or a spill at the gym is no concern. They are still not for swimming.</p>
<h2>Battery</h2>
<p>You can expect roughly a full working day of on-and-off listening, with the case bringing the total to about 24 hours with cancellation on. Five minutes in the case gives around an hour. The case charges by USB-C, MagSafe, Qi and an Apple Watch charger. Like all small lithium cells, the ones inside will fade after two or three years of daily use. Apple offers a battery service, at a price that is sometimes close to a discounted new pair.</p>
<h2>If you use Android</h2>
<p>They will connect as ordinary Bluetooth earbuds. Noise cancelling and Transparency work by squeezing the stem. You lose automatic switching, customisation, spatial audio, the fit test, Find My and easy firmware updates. At Rs 47,999 that is a poor bargain. Look at the <a href="/products/sony-wf-1000xm5">Sony WF-1000XM5</a> or the Galaxy Buds 4 Pro for Samsung phones.</p>
<h2>AirPods Pro 2 owners</h2>
<p>If your second-generation pair still lasts the day, the upgrade is nice and not necessary. You would gain stronger cancelling, better fit, improved water resistance and heart-rate sensing. If your battery is tired, this is the moment.</p>
<h2>What else is at this price?</h2>
[[rivals:airpods-pro-3]]
<p>For half the money, earbuds such as the <a href="/products/soundcore-liberty-5-pro">Soundcore Liberty 5 Pro</a> cancel noise impressively and work the same on every phone. They will not switch between your Apple devices by magic, and on a tight budget you may not care.</p>
<h2>Fakes are everywhere</h2>
<p>AirPods are among the most copied products in Nepal. Good copies show the same pairing animation and even a serial number that belongs to a real pair. Check properly. Open Settings on an iPhone with the earbuds connected and look for the full feature page, including the ear-tip fit test, Adaptive audio and firmware version. Run the fit test and switch between all three noise modes. Enter the serial number on Apple's coverage page and make sure the product, purchase date and warranty make sense. Look at the print quality on the case. If a seller asks Rs 8,000 for "original", it is not.</p>
<h2>Warranty in Nepal</h2>
<p>Buy from an authorised reseller and keep the bill. One-year service is handled through authorised providers, and they may replace a single bud instead of the full set. Register the pair to your Apple account right away, so that nobody else can use them if they are lost.</p>
<h2>Who should buy them</h2>
<ul>
<li>iPhone users who also own a Mac, iPad or Apple Watch.</li>
<li>Commuters who want excellent quiet without studying settings.</li>
<li>Runners who need water resistance and heart-rate data.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who should skip them</h2>
<ul>
<li>Android users.</li>
<li>People who lose earbuds. A Rs 5,000 pair hurts less.</li>
<li>Audiophiles who want high-bitrate wireless.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Smartwatch or fitness band: decide that first</h2>
<p>Many people who say they want a smartwatch need a fitness tracker, and the other way round. A fitness watch counts steps, shows notifications, records exercise and lasts a week or more. A full smartwatch, such as the Galaxy Watch 9, runs apps, replies to messages by voice or keyboard, plays music without the phone, gives turn-by-turn directions and takes calls properly. In return it wants a charger every day or two.</p>
<p>If you have never installed an app on a watch and only want health numbers, read our <a href="/reviews/huawei-watch-gt-6-review-nepal">Huawei Watch GT 6 review</a> before spending Rs 66,499.</p>
<h2>Speed and software</h2>
<p>The 3nm Snapdragon Wear Elite is a big change from earlier chips. Menus respond straight away, voice replies process quickly and maps draw without waiting. Wear OS 7 with Samsung's One UI on top gives access to the Play Store on the wrist. Google Maps, WhatsApp, Spotify, Strava and calculators all have proper apps. Google and Samsung's assistants understand English well. Nepali voice typing is limited, so most replies will be in English or by quick phrases.</p>
<h2>Screen and build</h2>
<p>Sapphire crystal is far harder than ordinary glass and resists scratches from walls, zips and keys. At 3,000 nits the AMOLED is readable in strong sun. At around 32 to 34 grams without the strap, both are light enough for sleep tracking.</p>
<p>The watch is rated 5ATM and IP68, which covers pools, showers with care and monsoon rain, and it meets a military standard for shock and temperature. Rinse it in fresh water after a pool, since chlorine eats at seals.</p>
<h2>Health tracking you can rely on, and what you cannot</h2>
<p>Heart rate, sleep stages, step counts, blood oxygen and skin temperature trends are useful when you look at weeks of data. The BioActive sensor estimates body fat and muscle using a small current. It is fine for seeing a direction of change, and not a medical reading. ECG and blood pressure depend on regional approval and on using a Samsung phone, and they may not be enabled in Nepal. No watch replaces a doctor. If it warns of an irregular rhythm or high heart rate at rest, get checked.</p>
<h2>GPS for runs and treks</h2>
<p>Dual-band GPS listens to two frequencies, so it copes better with tall buildings and deep valleys. Recorded distances in places like Thamel or on the Langtang trail should be closer to the truth than with single-band watches. Battery is the limit. Continuous GPS with the screen on will drain the watch in several hours, so it suits a day walk but not a week's trek without a power bank.</p>
<h2>Battery in practice</h2>
<p>About a day and a half with normal settings means a routine: charge while you shower and dress, and wear it at night for sleep tracking. Turn off the always-on display and it stretches towards two days. The charger is a small magnetic puck and should be packed for every trip. If a nightly charge sounds like a chore, this is not your watch.</p>
<h2>Which phones does it work with?</h2>
<p>It needs an Android phone. Any recent model will do, but some health features and the smoothest experience require a Samsung. It does not work with iPhones at all. Bluetooth and LTE versions exist in other markets. Check which one you are buying, as cellular watch plans are not a normal product in Nepal.</p>

<p>The older <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-watch-8">Galaxy Watch 8</a> sometimes sells for a few thousand rupees less. It has a slower chip and is otherwise similar, so it is worth buying only at a clear discount. The <a href="/products/huawei-watch-gt-7-pro">Huawei Watch GT 7 Pro</a> gives you premium materials and long battery life without apps. The <a href="/products/apple-watch-series-11">Apple Watch Series 11</a> is the equivalent for iPhone users. The <a href="/products/amazfit-t-rex-ultra">Amazfit T-Rex Ultra</a> is for people who go off-grid.</p>
<h2>Payments and local services</h2>
<p>Samsung Wallet and Google Wallet on the watch are attractive features abroad. In Nepal, tap-to-pay support from local banks is limited, so do not count on leaving your wallet at home. Notifications from eSewa, Khalti and banking apps arrive normally, which is handy for OTP messages.</p>
<h2>Everyday tips</h2>
<ul>
<li>Choose which apps may send notifications on the first day. Ten useful alerts beat two hundred.</li>

</ul>

<p>With a Samsung phone and a real interest in apps, replies and maps on your wrist, it is the best Android watch available here. With another Android phone it is still good, only a little less complete. With an iPhone, or with a dislike of daily charging, choose something else and keep Rs 20,000 or more in your pocket.</p>

=== moto-g64-review-nepal
<h2>Specifications</h2>
[[specs:moto-g64]]
<h2>First, check that it is still available</h2>
<p>The G64 went on sale in April 2024 and has since been marked as discontinued in our catalogue, so prices in this review are the last ones we recorded. You may still find sealed stock in some shops and plenty of used ones. If a seller asks the same Rs 29,999 today, compare it with newer phones first, because what made it special was how much it offered at that price two years ago. At a discount it remains a good phone.</p>
<h2>Why it became a favourite</h2>
<p>Budget phones usually cut the same corners: a low-resolution screen, 4GB of memory and slow storage. Motorola refused. The G64 has a sharp Full HD+ panel, 8GB or 12GB of memory, UFS storage and a chip with 5G. Anyone upgrading from a three-year-old budget phone feels the difference in the first minute, as apps open without the usual pause.</p>
<p>It is also one of the few phones in its class with optical stabilisation on the main camera. In dim rooms, that lets the phone use a slower shutter without blur. Budget rivals with the same 50MP number and no stabilisation produce softer pictures.</p>
<h2>Screen</h2>
<p>The 6.5-inch LCD is smooth at 120Hz and sharp enough for long reading. Brightness of around 560 nits is fine indoors and in shade, and you will tilt the phone to read it at midday. Being an LCD, it cannot show true black. If you watch films in a dark room, an AMOLED phone such as the <a href="/products/honor-x8d">Honor X8d</a> looks better.</p>
<h2>Performance today</h2>
<p>The Dimensity 7025 is a capable 6nm chip. Social apps, video calls, banking and office documents are smooth. PUBG Mobile and Free Fire run at medium settings. The 12GB version keeps many apps open, and since the price difference was only Rs 1,000, that is the one to look for.</p>
<h2>Battery</h2>
<p>With 6,000mAh, it lasts well over a day for most people, and the 30W charger fills it in a little less than two hours. A used phone from 2024 will have lost some capacity. Android 15 and later show battery health on some models. If yours does not, charge to full, use it for a normal day and see how much is left.</p>
<h2>Software: the honest weakness</h2>
<p>Motorola promised one Android upgrade and three years of security patches for the G64. It launched with Android 14, so the upgrade to 15 is the last version it will get, with security patches ending around 2027. That does not stop the phone working, but banking apps raise their minimum requirements over time. For someone who plans to use it until 2029, a Samsung with longer support is the safer choice.</p>
<p>In every other way the software is pleasant. It is close to plain Android, with few extra apps. The Moto gestures, a double chop for the torch and a double twist for the camera, are the kind of small idea you miss on other phones.</p>
<h2>Cameras</h2>
<p>The 50MP main camera is good in daylight and acceptable at night. The 8MP ultra wide has autofocus, so it also takes close-ups. The 16MP selfie camera is fine for calls and social media. Video tops out at 1080p, with steady results thanks to stabilisation.</p>
<h2>Durability and connectivity</h2>
<p>IP52 protects against dust and drips and nothing more. Carry it in a pouch during rain. There is a headphone jack, a microSD slot that shares space with the second SIM, side fingerprint reader and stereo speakers. For students, the headphone jack and card slot are more useful than they sound.</p>
<h2>What to buy instead in late 2026</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>For support:</strong> the <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a07">Galaxy A07</a>, slower but with a long update promise.</li>
<li><strong>For battery:</strong> the <a href="/products/tecno-spark-50">Tecno Spark 50</a> with 7,000mAh.</li>
<li><strong>For a better screen:</strong> spend a little more on the <a href="/products/honor-x8d">Honor X8d</a> with AMOLED.</li>
<li><strong>From the same brand:</strong> the <a href="/products/motorola-moto-g35-5g">Moto G35 5G</a>, a newer but simpler phone, or the <a href="/products/moto-g96">Moto G96</a> if you can reach Rs 45,000.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Buying a used G64</h2>
<p>Motorola's service presence in Nepal is smaller than Samsung's or Xiaomi's, so condition matters. Look for even brightness, test both speakers, take a photo in a dark room to check that stabilisation works, try both SIM slots and the card slot, and verify MDMS registration. Ask when the battery was last replaced, if ever. Expect to pay clearly less than the original price, and walk away from a unit with a third-party screen, which usually shows bluish whites and poor touch at the edges.</p>
<h2>Who should still consider it</h2>
<p>A student who needs a fast phone for little money and changes phones every two years. A second phone for hotspot use, where the battery and 5G modem help. Someone who likes clean Android and a headphone jack. For parents and long-term owners, choose a phone with more update years ahead.</p>
<h2>Final thoughts</h2>
<p>The Moto G64 set a standard that many newer phones at Rs 30,000 still fail to reach, which says more about the market than about the phone. Memory prices have risen, and manufacturers have gone back to 4GB and HD+ screens to hold the line. If you find one at a fair price in good condition, it is a smart buy. If you find one at full price, let it go.</p>

<p>The Galaxy A07 is rarely chosen by people who read spec sheets for fun. Spend ten minutes removing what you can. In the settings, turn off personalised recommendations and notifications from the app store, phone manager and theme apps. Keep Google Play as your only source of apps. Tecno's update policy for the Spark series is short, so expect security patches and perhaps one version upgrade.</p>
<h2>Who sells it and who fixes it</h2>
<p>Tecno, Infinix and itel belong to the same group, which has built a wide sales network in Nepal, including small towns. Carlcare is the group's service arm. Ask where the nearest service point is, and keep the bill. Screens and batteries for popular Spark models are usually easy to find.</p>
<h2>Compared with what Rs 30,000 buys elsewhere</h2>
[[rivals:tecno-spark-50]]
<p>The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a07s">Galaxy A07s</a> has a better-known brand and longer support with a smaller battery. The <a href="/products/motorola-moto-g35-5g">Moto G35 5G</a> adds 5G and a sharper screen. The <a href="/products/honor-x6d">Honor X6d</a> has a more efficient chip and slower charging. If you can find a <a href="/products/moto-g64">Moto G64</a> in old stock, it beats all of these for speed.</p>
<h2>Three kinds of buyer</h2>
<p><strong>The traveller or driver.</strong> Long days, few sockets, mostly calls and maps. The Spark 50 fits well.</p>
<p><strong>The student.</strong> Online classes, notes, social media and some games. The battery helps, but the slow chip will frustrate within a year. Choose the 6GB version or look at a phone with a Helio G99 or a Dimensity chip.</p>
<p><strong>The parent.</strong> A loud speaker, large screen and long battery are just right. Spend time on the setup to remove clutter and scam risks.</p>
<h2>Getting the best from the battery</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use 60Hz mode if you do not notice the difference. It adds hours.</li>
<li>Keep the screen at automatic brightness.</li>
<li>Charge with the supplied adapter and avoid cheap cables that slow it further.</li>
<li>Try not to keep it at 100 per cent on the charger all day.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Things we would like to see improved</h2>
<p>A faster charger would change how this phone feels more than anything else. After that, a Full HD+ screen and a 6nm chip would put it level with rivals. None of those are possible after purchase, so decide whether you can live with them now.</p>
<h2>Final advice</h2>
<p>Buy the Spark 50 if your first question about any phone is how long the battery lasts. In that respect it beats everything near it. === honor-x6d-review-nepal
<h2>Specifications</h2>
[[specs:honor-x6d]]
<h2>A phone that arrived at the wrong price</h2>
<p>There is nothing wrong with the Honor X6d as an object. It is well assembled, light, up to date and efficient. The problem is simple arithmetic. At Rs 29,999 a buyer can have more memory, a sharper screen, faster charging or a bigger battery from someone else. A review has to judge a phone against what else your money could do, and that is why the score is low while the phone itself is perfectly usable.</p>
<h2>What works</h2>
<p>The Dimensity 6300 is a 6nm chip with 5G, and it is the best part. It opens everyday apps without long pauses, stays cool and gets good mileage from the battery. In a price band full of 12nm processors, that is a meaningful advantage. If you place it beside a <a href="/products/tecno-spark-50">Tecno Spark 50</a> and open the same five apps, the Honor finishes first.</p>
<p>It also runs MagicOS 10 on Android 16, the newest version you will see on a budget phone this year. Google services are all present. Honor's interface is tidy compared with others at this price, with fewer adverts. Useful touches include a sidebar for opening a second app in a small window and a simple mode for older users.</p>
<p>At 186 grams it feels lighter than most big-battery rivals, and the 6.75-inch screen fills the front nicely.</p>
<h2>What does not</h2>
<p>Four gigabytes of memory in a phone sold in late 2026 is the main limit. Android itself, the Google services and the keyboard use most of it, so switching between WhatsApp, a browser and the camera means waiting for apps to reload. There is no 6GB version listed in Nepal.</p>
<p>The LCD is HD+ with a peak of about 450 nits. Indoors that is fine. In the afternoon sun at a bus stop you will be shading it with your palm. Charging at 15W is the slowest on the market, taking well over two hours for a full charge, and it makes little sense when rivals offer 30W.</p>
<p>The main 50MP camera is respectable in daylight, and the 5MP selfie camera is the weakest in the group. Video calls in a dim room will look rough.</p>
<h2>Battery life</h2>
<p>The numbers are average and the result is better than average, because the chip and the low-resolution screen use so little. A day and a half of calls, chat and video is realistic. It is the refilling that tests your patience.</p>
<h2>5G: the argument in its favour</h2>
<p>If you want a light 5G phone from a known brand at about Rs 30,000, the list is short. The X6d is on it, as is the <a href="/products/motorola-moto-g35-5g">Moto G35 5G</a>. Whether 5G is worth paying for is another matter. Coverage in Nepal is limited and mobile data packs are small, so most owners will run on 4G for years.</p>
<h2>How it compares</h2>
[[rivals:honor-x6d]]
<p>The Motorola gives you a sharper, smoother screen and near-stock Android. The Tecno and ZTE models give you larger batteries. Samsung's <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a07s">Galaxy A07s</a> brings longer software support and a wider service network. A leftover <a href="/products/moto-g64">Moto G64</a> has twice the memory. Each of them is a better answer to a specific need.</p>
<h2>When it becomes a good buy</h2>
<p>Phones in this class often fall in price within months, and shops run bundles. Here is a guide to when it makes sense:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Around Rs 29,999:</strong> skip it.</li>
<li><strong>Around Rs 26,000:</strong> fair, if you value a light body and 5G.</li>
<li><strong>Under Rs 24,000:</strong> a good phone for parents and light users.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Service and warranty</h2>
<p>Honor is growing in Nepal, but its network remains smaller than those of Samsung, Xiaomi and vivo. Find out the nearest service point and the price of a screen before buying. Check the IMEI in MDMS, and insist on a sealed box with the distributor's warranty card.</p>
<h2>If you already own one</h2>
<p>You can make it noticeably nicer to live with. Uninstall apps you do not use, especially those that run in the background. Use the lite versions of Facebook and Messenger. Restart the phone once a week to clear memory. Keep at least 15GB of storage free. Turn the refresh rate to 90Hz if scrolling feels rough, or 60Hz to save power. Buy a longer charging cable and charge overnight, since the slow speed is then no bother and is even gentle on the battery.</p>
<h2>Who might still choose it</h2>
<p>Someone who wants a light, tidy phone for calls, messaging and occasional video, likes Honor's software and has found a discount. Someone who needs 5G support for an office SIM or a hotspot on a budget. For games, photos or a phone to keep for four years, look elsewhere.</p>
<h2>The wider lesson</h2>
<p>Budget phones have become harder to judge by their price alone. Two phones at Rs 30,000 can be separated by years in speed and comfort. When comparing, look at four numbers before anything else: memory, screen resolution, charging speed and the age of the chip's process. The X6d wins one of those four. Most rivals win two or three.</p>

<h2>Specifications</h2>

<h2>What you notice first</h2>
<p>Pick up the Y05 and two things stand out for a phone at about Rs 25,000. The screen is easy to see, and the body feels solid. A 6.74-inch panel with a 1,200-nit high-brightness mode is rare at this price. Walk outside and you can read a message without cupping your hand over it. <h2>The infrared blaster deserves its own paragraph</h2>
<p>It sounds like a relic, yet an IR transmitter is useful in Nepali homes. The phone can act as a remote for a television, set-top box, fan or air conditioner. <p>At 6,500mAh, with a low-resolution screen and an undemanding chip, two days are possible for light users and a full day is certain for heavy ones. The downside is 15W charging. It needs about three hours from empty, so overnight charging is the sensible routine. vivo says its batteries are designed to keep most of their capacity for several years, and slow charging does no harm there.</p>
<h2>Where the savings were made</h2>
<p>The Unisoc T7225 is an entry-level chip on a 12nm process. Opening the camera takes a moment, the keyboard sometimes trails your typing, and a heavy Facebook feed stutters. With 4GB of memory, apps in the background are closed quickly. The storage is eMMC, which is slower than the UFS storage in better phones, and that shows when installing or updating apps.</p>
<p>The main camera has 8 megapixels. In good light it takes pictures that look acceptable on a phone screen. It is fine for photographing a document, a notice or a bill. Evening photos of people are blurry and noisy. The 5MP front camera is for video calls only.</p>
<h2>64GB or 128GB</h2>
<p>The gap is about Rs 3,500. Take 128GB. With 64GB, after the system and a few large apps, you will have perhaps 35GB for everything else, and WhatsApp will claim most of that within a year. When a phone's storage is nearly full, it slows down, and on this chip you cannot afford that.</p>
<h2>Software</h2>
<p>OriginOS 6 on Android 16 is a welcome change from older vivo systems, with a cleaner design. There are preinstalled apps and a second app store. Remove what you do not need, and turn off suggestions in settings. vivo's update plan for the cheapest Y phones is limited, so regard the version you get as close to the one you will keep, with security patches for a time.</p>
<h2>Who it is really for</h2>
<p>We think of three people. The first is a parent or grandparent who wants a bright, loud phone with a battery that lasts. The second is a worker who needs a tough-ish phone for calls, WhatsApp groups, eSewa and YouTube at lunch. The third is a family that wants a spare phone and a universal remote in one.</p>
<p>It is not for students who run many apps at once, not for gamers, and not for anyone who likes taking photos.</p>

[[rivals:vivo-y05]]
