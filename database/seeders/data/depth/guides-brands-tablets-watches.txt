=== apple-tablet-price-in-nepal
<h2>iPads compared</h2>
[[models:ipad-11-2025,apple-ipad-air-11-m4-2026,apple-ipad-air-13-m4-2026,apple-ipad-pro-11-m5-2025,apple-ipad-pro-13-m5-2025]]
<h2>Why iPads outlast everything else</h2>
<p>An iPad bought in 2018 still opens, still updates most apps and still sells for a third of its price. No Android tablet in Nepal can say that. The reasons are long software support, chips that are far more powerful than tablets need, and apps made for a large screen. If you plan to keep a tablet for five years or more, an iPad is often the cheaper choice, though the sticker says otherwise.</p>
<h2>iPad 11: enough for most people</h2>
<p>At about Rs 83,500 the basic <a href="/products/ipad-11-2025">iPad</a> has the A16 chip, an 11-inch screen and 128GB. It handles notes, films, drawing lessons, video calls and games. It lacks Apple Intelligence, the laminated display and the better pencil. For a school or college student who will read, annotate and watch, it is the right one, and spending more should be a deliberate choice.</p>
<h2>iPad Air: for people who make things</h2>
<p>The Air at about Rs 1,40,000 adds the M4, more memory, a laminated screen that removes the gap under the glass, and the Apple Pencil Pro with squeeze and hover. Illustrators, architecture students and video editors feel these every day. The 13-inch Air is for people who want a whole A4 page on the screen. If you only take notes, you will not notice most of it.</p>
<h2>iPad Pro: for professionals</h2>
<p>Tandem OLED, 120Hz, M5 and a thinness that surprises. At Rs 2,29,500 and above before accessories, it competes with a MacBook Air plus a basic iPad. Choose it if you are paid to draw, grade colour or edit on location.</p>
<h2>The accessories question</h2>
<p>Prices of Apple's accessories change the whole calculation. There are three pencils, and each works with certain iPads only:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Apple Pencil (USB-C):</strong> cheapest, no pressure sensitivity. Good for notes.</li>
<li><strong>Apple Pencil Pro:</strong> pressure, squeeze, hover. For Air and Pro.</li>
<li><strong>Older pencils:</strong> for older iPads. Check compatibility.</li>
</ul>
<p>Third-party pencils at Rs 2,500 to 5,000 work well for writing, with palm rejection and no pressure. For lecture notes they are the sensible purchase. Keyboards from other makers at Rs 5,000 to 10,000 do most of what the Magic Keyboard does.</p>
<h2>Will it replace a laptop?</h2>
<p>For email, documents, presentations and browsing, with a keyboard, yes. For Excel beyond the basics, programming, accounting software and many government portals, no. An Air with Apple's keyboard and pencil costs more than a MacBook Neo and nearly as much as a good Windows laptop. Read <a href="/guides/laptop-vs-tablet-work-study-nepal">laptop or tablet</a> before deciding.</p>
<h2>Storage</h2>
<p>128GB is comfortable for study. Procreate files, 4K video and offline films fill it. There is no card slot, but USB-C drives work in the Files app. Choose 256GB if you edit video or if children share it.</p>
<h2>Wi-Fi or cellular?</h2>
<p>Cellular versions cost about Rs 20,000 to 30,000 more. A phone hotspot does the same job for most. Cellular iPads also have GPS and need MDMS registration. Recent models use eSIM only, so confirm with your operator.</p>
<h2>Buying official or imported</h2>
<p>Apple's limited warranty on iPads is usually honoured through authorised service in many countries, and cellular models are the exception to check. Enter the serial number on Apple's coverage page. American, Emirati and Indian units are all common in Nepal. Screen replacement is very costly, so a case is essential.</p>
<h2>Used iPads</h2>
<p>A used iPad Air M1 at Rs 60,000 to 75,000 or an iPad 9 or 10 at Rs 30,000 to 50,000 is a good buy. Check:</p>
<ul>
<li>Activation Lock is off. Erase it and set it up yourself.</li>
<li>No remote management from a school or company.</li>
<li>Touch works in every corner. Drag an icon along all edges.</li>
<li>The pencil connects and charges.</li>
<li>Battery lasts as the seller claims. Watch video for twenty minutes.</li>
<li>The frame is straight. Lay it on a table.</li>
</ul>
<h2>For children</h2>
<p>Use Screen Time with a passcode, Guided Access to lock a child into one app, and Ask to Buy for purchases. Buy a thick foam case. A base iPad, used or new, outlives several cheap tablets and is safer on the internet, because you control which apps are allowed.</p>
<h2>For artists</h2>
<p>Procreate is a single purchase and remains the best reason to own an iPad. Start with the base iPad and the cheapest compatible pencil that has pressure sensitivity, if your model supports one, or a used Air with the second-generation Pencil. A matte protector helps the hand and softens the picture.</p>
<h2>For students of medicine and science</h2>
<p>GoodNotes, Notability and Apple's own Notes app with handwriting search change how revision works. Split view with a PDF on one side and notes on the other is the best study tool we know. The base iPad is enough. Buy the 13-inch Air only if you read two-column papers all day.</p>
<h2>iPadOS and updates</h2>
<p>Expect five to seven years of new versions and some years of patches after that. Newer iPadOS versions have proper windows on most recent models. Features tied to Apple Intelligence need an M-series chip or newer A-series with enough memory.</p>
<h2>Battery and care</h2>
<p>About ten hours of use is typical. Keep it between 20 and 80 per cent when convenient, and set the 80 per cent limit on newer models if it is always on a stand. Do not leave it in a hot car. Clean the screen with a damp microfibre cloth.</p>
<h2>When to buy</h2>
<p>New iPads appear irregularly, often in spring and autumn. In 2026 memory costs pushed prices up, so clearance is modest. Look for older stock of the previous Air, and ask about education prices at authorised resellers.</p>
<h2>Our shortlist</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Most people and students:</strong> iPad 11, 128GB.</li>
<li><strong>Artists and architects:</strong> iPad Air 11.</li>
<li><strong>Large documents and music scores:</strong> iPad Air 13.</li>
<li><strong>Professionals:</strong> iPad Pro 11.</li>
<li><strong>Tight budget:</strong> used iPad 10 or Air M1.</li>
</ul>

The <a href="/products/xiaomi-pad-7">Xiaomi Pad 7</a> has a smoother screen for half the price. They are good tablets with shorter lives and fewer large-screen apps. <h2>Offline maps</h2>

<h2>Heart rate and health</h2>
<p>Good at rest and steady pace. Cold hands at altitude reduce accuracy for all optical sensors, so wear the watch over a thin sleeve or use a chest strap. SpO2 readings at altitude are indicative. Use a fingertip oximeter for decisions, and descend if you feel unwell, whatever the watch says.</p>

<h2>Used Amazfits</h2>
