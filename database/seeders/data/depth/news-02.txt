<p>A laptop is a box for moving heat. A thick box with two large fans and a liquid metal compound can let an RTX 5080 draw 150 to 175 watts. A 1.85kg aluminium body cannot. === wifi-7-adoption-nepal-routers-speed-isp-support
<h2>What Wi-Fi 7 adds</h2>
<p>Three changes matter. Channels can be twice as wide, up to 320MHz, in the 6GHz band. Devices can use two bands at once, called multi-link operation, which reduces delay and dropouts. And data is packed more densely when the signal is strong. In a laboratory that produces astonishing numbers. In a home, the second change is the one people would feel: steadier video calls and games.</p>
<p>There is a catch. The widest channels need the 6GHz band, and each country decides whether and how it may be used. Check what the regulator in Nepal permits before paying for a router whose main advantage depends on it. Without 6GHz, Wi-Fi 7 works on 2.4 and 5GHz and is only modestly better than Wi-Fi 6.</p>
<h2>Your plan is the ceiling</h2>
<p>If your fibre package is 100Mbps, every generation of Wi-Fi from the last ten years can carry it across a room. A faster standard will not make a download quicker. It may make it reach the bedroom better, and that depends on walls. Most Nepali households have packages between 80 and 300Mbps. Wi-Fi 6 carries those comfortably.</p>
<h2>Concrete and brick: the real problem</h2>
<p>Houses here are built of brick and reinforced concrete. Each wall takes away a large part of a 5GHz signal, and a floor slab takes more. 6GHz is worse still. So a router in the corner of the ground floor cannot serve a room on the third, whatever the number on its box. The cure is placement and more access points.</p>
<h3>Placement rules</h3>
<ul>
<li>In the middle of the home, high on a shelf, in the open.</li>
<li>Away from the television, microwave, fridge and aquarium.</li>
<li>Not inside the metal box with the fibre terminal.</li>
<li>Antennas at different angles if you have floors above and below.</li>
</ul>
<p>Try this first. Moving a router two metres can double the speed in the next room, free of charge.</p>
<h2>Mesh, extender or cable?</h2>
<p><strong>A cable</strong> from the router to a second access point is best, and costs little during construction or painting. If you are building a house, run Ethernet to every floor. You will thank yourself for thirty years.</p>
<p><strong>Mesh</strong> systems with two or three units are the easiest cure for a finished house. Choose one with a dedicated wireless link, or connect units by cable if you can. A Wi-Fi 6 mesh is enough.</p>
<p><strong>Extenders</strong> are cheap, and halve the speed. Use them for a doorbell or a distant light.</p>
<p><strong>Powerline adapters</strong> are a gamble with older wiring.</p>
<h2>The router from your ISP</h2>
<p>It is usually basic, locked, and shared with the fibre terminal. You can often keep it as the terminal and connect your own router behind it. Ask the ISP to put theirs in bridge mode, or turn off its Wi-Fi and use yours as an access point. If two routers both hand out addresses, some things such as games and cameras will misbehave. Support staff know the setting as "bridge" or "double NAT".</p>
<h2>How to measure properly</h2>
<ol>
<li>Connect a laptop by cable to the router and run a speed test. This is your plan's real speed.</li>
<li>Repeat on 5GHz Wi-Fi beside the router. It should be close.</li>
<li>Repeat in each room. Write it down.</li>
<li>Repeat at nine in the evening. If the cable result is also slow, the problem is the ISP and no router will help.</li>
</ol>
<h2>What to buy by situation</h2>
<p><strong>Flat, 100Mbps, few devices.</strong> Keep the ISP router and move it.</p>
<p><strong>Three-storey house, 150 to 300Mbps.</strong> Wi-Fi 6 mesh with two or three units.</p>
<p><strong>Gaming.</strong> A cable to the PC or console. Nothing wireless is as steady.</p>
<p><strong>Home office with video calls.</strong> Wi-Fi 6 router near the desk and a small UPS for router and terminal.</p>
<p><strong>500Mbps or more, new devices, NAS.</strong> Here Wi-Fi 7 starts to make sense.</p>
<p><strong>Small office.</strong> Proper access points on the ceiling with wired connections, from a business range.</p>
<h2>A small UPS is the best network upgrade</h2>
<p>Routers and fibre terminals use 10 to 20 watts together. A mini UPS for Rs 2,500 to 4,500 keeps them on for hours in a power cut. Fibre networks usually stay up. This one purchase improves life more than any new Wi-Fi standard.</p>
<h2>Which devices have Wi-Fi 7?</h2>
<p>Recent flagship phones, many laptops from 2025 onwards and the newest Macs. They work with older routers, so there is no hurry. When you replace a router in two or three years because it has failed, Wi-Fi 7 models will be the ordinary choice and cost less.</p>
<h2>Wi-Fi 6E: a stop on the way</h2>
<p>Wi-Fi 6E is Wi-Fi 6 with the 6GHz band. If you see it at a good price, the same question applies: is 6GHz permitted and useful in your home? For most homes, plain Wi-Fi 6 is the same thing for less.</p>
<h2>Security settings worth five minutes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Change the router's admin password, not only the Wi-Fi password.</li>
<li>Use WPA2/WPA3 and a long passphrase.</li>
<li>Create a guest network for visitors and smart devices.</li>
<li>Turn off WPS and remote management.</li>
<li>Update the firmware twice a year.</li>
<li>Check the list of connected devices and remove strangers.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Common myths</h2>
<p><strong>"More antennas mean more speed."</strong> Not by themselves. <strong>"5GHz is always better."</strong> Near the router, yes. Two rooms away, 2.4GHz may win. <strong>"Wi-Fi 7 is dangerous."</strong> It uses low power, like every version before it. <strong>"A booster app will help."</strong> No app can.</p>
<h2>When we would upgrade to Wi-Fi 7</h2>
<p>If we were buying a new router anyway, had a plan above 500Mbps, owned at least two Wi-Fi 7 devices, and could use 6GHz in the room where we work. If three of those are true, go ahead. If one is true, buy a good Wi-Fi 6 mesh and spend the difference on a UPS and an Ethernet cable.</p>
<h2>Our advice</h2>
<p>Move the router, measure the rooms, add a cable or a mesh unit, and protect the power. Do these in order and stop when the problem is gone. In most Nepali homes it will be gone before you reach the shop that sells Wi-Fi 7.</p>

<h2>What a price in Nepal is made of</h2>
<p>To understand why a phone costs what it does, open it up. Suppose a mid-range phone leaves the factory gate at 300 dollars. Freight and insurance add a little. Customs duty and any excise are charged on that value, then VAT at 13 per cent on the total. The distributor adds a margin to cover warranty, marketing and the cost of money. The dealer adds another. By the time it is on the counter, the rupee figure is far above a plain conversion.</p>
<p>Two conclusions follow. A small rise at the factory is multiplied on the way. And a weaker rupee raises every one of those steps at once. In 2026 both happened.</p>
<h2>Memory: the largest single cause</h2>
<p>Memory and storage can be a fifth or more of the parts cost of a budget phone and a large share of a laptop. When they rose, makers had four choices: raise the price, reduce the memory, cut something else, or accept less profit. We have seen the first three. If a successor arrives with a smaller base storage, a missing charger or an older chip at the same price, that is also a price rise. It is just harder to see. Our article on <a href="/news/electronic-price-hikes-nepal-ram-ssd-smartphones-analysis">RAM and SSD prices</a> explains the mechanism.</p>
<h2>Who was hit hardest</h2>
<p><strong>Buyers under Rs 40,000.</strong> Memory is a bigger share of cheap phones. Some models that started at Rs 20,000 in 2025 now begin at Rs 25,000 with the same specification.</p>
<p><strong>Students buying laptops.</strong> The 16GB machines that used to be Rs 85,000 moved towards a lakh, and 8GB returned to the shelves.</p>
<p><strong>PC builders.</strong> Memory, SSDs and graphics cards all rose together.</p>
<p><strong>Console buyers.</strong> For the first time in memory, an ageing console costs more than it did at launch.</p>
<h2>Who was hardly affected</h2>
<p>People buying earbuds, watches, power banks, basic monitors and older televisions. Competition there is fierce and memory content is small. If you were postponing a pair of headphones because "everything is expensive", you need not.</p>
<h2>Why prices do not fall quickly</h2>
<p>Prices rise by email and fall by clearance. A distributor who imported a thousand phones at a high cost will not sell them below it unless forced. So even when parts become cheaper, shelves need months to turn over. Retailers also remember: once customers accept Rs 45,000 for a certain phone, nobody hurries back to Rs 37,000. The pressure comes from a rival with newer, cheaper stock, and that is why watching several brands helps.</p>
<h2>How to buy well in an expensive year</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Buy less often.</strong> A new battery for Rs 4,000 to 8,000 gives a phone two more years.</li>
<li><strong>Buy one step down, with more memory.</strong> An A-series phone with 8GB/256GB ages better than a fancier one with 6GB/128GB.</li>
<li><strong>Buy last year's flagship.</strong> Its price falls while new mid-range prices rise. The two lines have crossed.</li>
<li><strong>Use festivals, with a notebook.</strong> Record the price before. Our <a href="/blog/dashain-tihar-tech-deals-how-to-spot-a-real-discount">festival deals article</a> shows how.</li>
<li><strong>Ask for exchange and bank offers together.</strong> They sometimes combine.</li>
<li><strong>Compare across brands.</strong> Increases were uneven. One brand's rise is another's opportunity.</li>
</ol>
<h2>New, used and refurbished in 2026</h2>
<p>Used prices rose too, since demand moved there, but by less. Business laptops and two-year-old flagships are the sweet spots. Refurbished goods depend wholly on who did the work. Insist on a warranty of at least three months, a battery health figure and, for phones, MDMS registration. <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">How to check</a>.</p>
<h2>Is a grey import worth the saving now?</h2>
<p>When official prices rise, the gap to grey units widens and the temptation grows. Consider the whole cost. No local warranty, uncertain registration and weaker resale. On a Rs 40,000 phone the saving is rarely worth it. On a Rs 2 lakh phone it may be Rs 25,000, and some people take that risk knowingly. If you do, verify the IMEI before paying, and do not call it a bargain until the first year has passed.</p>
<h2>EMI in a year of rising prices</h2>
<p>Instalments make a high price feel small. Zero per cent plans are useful if the cash price is the same, because your money keeps working for you. If the plan carries a fee or interest, or removes a discount, you are paying more for a product that is already expensive. Ask for the total, and keep the monthly amount under a tenth of your income.</p>
<h2>For shops and small businesses</h2>
<p>If you buy equipment, ask suppliers to hold quotations for a month. Standardise on one laptop model. Consider desktops, which give more performance and can be upgraded. Stretch replacement cycles from three to four years with an SSD and a memory upgrade. And keep a spare machine, because emergencies are when people pay the most.</p>
<h2>Do official announcements help?</h2>
<p>Government decisions on customs and VAT, usually with the annual budget in Jestha and Asar, can move prices more than any factory. A change in duty on phones or computers shows up within weeks on new stock. If you read that a rate will change, ask your dealer whether current stock was cleared under the old one. It can work in your favour in either direction.</p>
<h2>What we expect next</h2>
<p>We are careful with predictions. New memory capacity is being built, and it takes time. Flagship prices in early 2027 look more likely to rise than fall, judging by reports on the coming Galaxy and iPhone models. Budget phones may stabilise if competition among Xiaomi, Realme, Infinix and Tecno continues. Consoles depend on Sony and Nintendo. Laptops with 16GB should remain around or above a lakh for some time.</p>

<h2>A calmer way to think about it</h2>
<p>A gadget is expensive when it is replaced early and cheap when it lasts. Divide any price by the months you will use it. A Rs 45,000 phone over four years is under Rs 940 a month. A Rs 98,000 laptop over five years is about Rs 1,630. Seen that way, choosing well and looking after it will save you more than any discount 2026 has to offer.</p>
