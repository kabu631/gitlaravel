<h2>Cheap earbuds are no longer bad earbuds</h2>
<p>Five years ago Rs 2,000 bought you a pair that disconnected when you turned your head. The chips inside budget earbuds have since become stable, batteries have doubled and Bluetooth 5.3 is normal. What has not changed is that makers still have to save somewhere. They save on microphones, on the tuning of the sound and on how long the parts last. Choose with those three in mind and you can be very happy for the price of a family lunch.</p>
<h2>Sound: expect bass, look for balance</h2>
<p>Almost every budget pair is tuned with heavy bass, because it impresses in a shop. After a week it hides voices and tires the ear. A companion app with an equaliser fixes this. Reduce the lowest two sliders a little and raise the middle, and speech and Nepali songs become much clearer. Soundcore, Realme and Redmi have usable apps. Pairs with no app leave you with whatever the maker liked.</p>
<h2>Noise cancelling at Rs 2,600: what to believe</h2>
<p>The Kick Phantom Buds X Pro and Realme Buds T200x advertise ANC. It is real and modest. Engine rumble on a bus drops by perhaps a third or a half, while horns and voices come through. A better seal from the right ear tips gives as much benefit again. If you have tried premium earbuds before, you will be underwhelmed. If you have not, you will notice the difference the first time you turn it off.</p>
<h2>Calls</h2>
<p>Indoors, all of these are fine for a short call. Outside, wind and traffic defeat them. If you work through phone calls, a wired earphone with a microphone on the cable still beats any wireless pair under Rs 5,000, and costs Rs 500.</p>
<h2>Reading the battery claim</h2>
<p>The big number on the box includes the case. The useful number is hours per charge for the buds themselves: the Soundcore P20i lists ten hours, and many others give five to seven with ANC on. Quick charging, ten minutes for an hour or two, is more useful than a total of 75 hours that you will never test.</p>
<h2>Who should choose which</h2>
<p><strong>Daily commuter:</strong> Kick Phantom Buds X Pro, for ANC and connection to two devices.</p>
<p><strong>Student with one phone and a small budget:</strong> Redmi Buds 5A. Simple and reliable, and Xiaomi has service points.</p>
<p><strong>Laptop and phone user:</strong> Realme Buds T200x, for multipoint and a balanced sound.</p>
<p><strong>Someone who forgets to charge:</strong> Soundcore P20i or boAt Airdopes 212.</p>
<p><strong>Gym:</strong> any with IPX4 or better and a snug fit. Try them while shaking your head.</p>
<h2>Fit, the free upgrade</h2>
<p>Open the packet of spare tips. Put in each size, play a song with bass and choose the one that sounds fullest without pressure. Many people need a different size in each ear. If none seals, buy a set of memory foam tips for a few hundred rupees. No other change improves cheap earbuds as much.</p>
<h2>Fakes</h2>
<p>At this price, counterfeits are common in markets and on social media pages. They copy AirPods, JBL and boAt. Signs include a price too good to be true, spelling mistakes on the box, no importer sticker, and a name in the Bluetooth list that does not match. Buy from a shop that gives a bill, and check the warranty card for a distributor's name.</p>
<h2>How long will they last?</h2>
<p>Two years is a fair expectation. Tiny batteries fade, the case hinge loosens, and one bud may go quiet when wax blocks the mesh. Clean the mesh with a dry, soft brush once a month. Do not leave the case in a hot car. Warranty is six months to a year. Some shops exchange a faulty bud, others repair only the case. Ask.</p>
<h2>Earbuds or a neckband?</h2>
<p>A neckband at the same price has a bigger battery, better call microphones and cannot be lost down a drain. It looks less tidy and catches on collars. For delivery riders, shopkeepers and anyone on calls all day, a neckband is the sensible choice.</p>
<h2>Settings that help</h2>
<ul>
<li>Turn on game mode only when gaming, as it can reduce quality.</li>
<li>Update firmware in the app on the first day.</li>
<li>Disable double-tap functions you trigger by accident.</li>
<li>Use one bud at a time on long calls to double the battery.</li>
<li>Keep volume under 60 per cent.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Frequently asked</h2>
<p><strong>Will they work with an iPhone?</strong> Yes. Apps and codecs are limited, but AAC is supported on most.</p>
<p><strong>Can I use them with a TV?</strong> With any Bluetooth TV, though there may be a delay in some apps.</p>
<p><strong>Is a higher driver size better?</strong> Not by itself. Tuning and fit matter more.</p>
<p><strong>Are they safe in rain?</strong> IPX4 and IPX5 handle sweat and drizzle. The case normally has no protection.</p>
<h2>Should you spend Rs 2,000 more?</h2>
<p>At Rs 4,500 to 5,000, noise cancelling becomes useful, calls improve and the apps are better. If you listen for more than an hour a day, see <a href="/guides/best-earbuds-under-5000-nepal">earbuds under Rs 5,000</a>. If these are a spare pair or a gift for a teenager who loses things, stay here.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>The Kick Phantom Buds X Pro gives the most for the money, the Redmi Buds 5A is the most sensible cheap pair, and the P20i is for those who value battery and an app. Any of them is better than the unboxed "AirPods" on the footpath, which cost nearly as much.</p>

<h2>Start with a notebook, not a shop</h2>
<p>Write down what annoyed you this month. Missed calls in the kitchen? A phone that comes out of your pocket forty times a day? Poor sleep? A running route you could not record? Each of those points to a feature. A list of three annoyances is a better shopping guide than a list of thirty features.</p>
<h2>The four families of watch</h2>
<p><strong>Bands.</strong> Slim, light, a week or two of battery, Rs 3,500 to 9,000. Excellent for sleep and steps. The <a href="/products/galaxy-fit-3">Galaxy Fit 3</a> and <a href="/products/huawei-band-10">Huawei Band 10</a> are typical.</p>
<p><strong>Fitness watches.</strong> Larger screen, GPS from about Rs 10,000, ten days or more of battery. Redmi, CMF, Amazfit and Huawei. They show messages but cannot run many apps.</p>
<p><strong>Smartwatches.</strong> Galaxy Watch, Apple Watch and Wear OS models. Replies, apps, maps and assistants. One to two days of battery. New ones start at about Rs 22,000 with the Galaxy Watch FE.</p>
<p><strong>Sports and expedition watches.</strong> Garmin, Coros, Amazfit T-Rex and Huawei Ultimate. Long GPS life, offline maps, physical buttons. From Rs 30,000 to more than a lakh.</p>
<p>Nearly every disappointment comes from buying one family and expecting another.</p>
<h2>Your phone decides half of it</h2>
<p>An Apple Watch works only with an iPhone. A Galaxy Watch works only with Android and is best with a Samsung. Others work with both, but on an iPhone none of them can reply to messages. If you plan to change phone brand next year, choose a watch that does not care.</p>
<h2>Features explained without marketing</h2>
<p><strong>GPS.</strong> Built-in records your route without a phone. Connected uses the phone. Dual-band is more accurate among buildings and trees.</p>
<p><strong>AMOLED.</strong> Brighter, with an always-on option. Worth paying for.</p>
<p><strong>Heart rate.</strong> Good at rest, variable in exercise.</p>
<p><strong>SpO2.</strong> A rough indication. A fingertip device is better for trekking.</p>
<p><strong>ECG.</strong> On premium watches, sometimes disabled by region.</p>
<p><strong>Barometer.</strong> Counts climb and warns of weather changes.</p>
<p><strong>NFC.</strong> Contactless payment support in Nepal is limited, so do not pay extra for it.</p>
<p><strong>LTE.</strong> Watches with a SIM need operator support. Check before assuming that it will work here.</p>
<h2>What Nepali conditions ask for</h2>
<p>Bright sun, dust, sweat, rain, and long days away from a socket. That means at least 800 nits, 5ATM water resistance, a strap that dries quickly and a battery of five days or more. For hills, a barometer and dual-band GPS. For motorbike commuters, vibration strong enough to feel through a jacket.</p>
<h2>Reading accuracy claims</h2>
<p>Every maker says its sensor is precise. In practice, accuracy depends on fit. Wear it snugly, a finger's width above the wrist bone, with clean skin. Warm up before judging heart rate. Use trends over weeks and not single readings. Ignore calories. And never use a watch to diagnose yourself.</p>
<h2>Battery life, real figures</h2>
<p>Divide the maker's number by two for always-on use with daily exercise. If the result is still above five days, you will not think about charging. If it is under two, decide when you will charge it every day: many people use the time they shower and dress.</p>
<h2>Size and comfort</h2>
<p>Watches under 35 grams disappear. Above 50 grams you notice them in bed. Case sizes near 42mm suit most wrists. Try on the actual watch, as lug shape matters. If you have sensitive skin, look for titanium or plastic backs and a fabric strap.</p>

<h2>The three prices that matter</h2>
[[models:redmi-buds-5a,kick-phantom-buds-x-pro,oneplus-nord-buds-4,airpods-4,airpods-pro-3,sony-wf-1000xm6]]
<p>The gap between Rs 2,000 and Rs 5,000 is enormous. The gap between Rs 5,000 and Rs 15,000 is clear. The gap between Rs 15,000 and Rs 65,000 is for people who know exactly what they are paying for. Decide which of these you are, and most of the noise disappears.</p>
<h2>Where do you listen?</h2>
<p><strong>On a bus or bike taxi.</strong> Noise cancelling and a secure fit. For bikes, remember that blocking traffic is dangerous if you are the one riding.</p>
<p><strong>At a desk.</strong> Comfort and multipoint. Consider headphones too, as we explain in <a href="/blog/earbuds-vs-headphones-which-should-you-buy">earbuds or headphones</a>.</p>
<p><strong>In the gym or on a run.</strong> Ear hooks or wing tips, IPX4 or more, physical buttons.</p>
<p><strong>In bed.</strong> Small, flat buds, or a sleep headband. Ordinary buds press when you lie on your side.</p>
<p><strong>In meetings.</strong> Microphones first. Test them.</p>
<h2>Fit: how to get it right</h2>
<p>Insert the bud, rotate it slightly back, and smile. If it loosens, use a larger tip. If it aches after ten minutes, use a smaller one. Play a tone with bass: with a good seal, it is full. Without one, it is thin. Two ears may need two sizes. Foam tips seal better and last about six months. If nothing seals, choose an open design such as AirPods 4 and accept more outside noise.</p>
<h2>Noise cancelling by the numbers, and why to distrust them</h2>
<p>Boxes say 35dB, 48dB or 55dB. These are maximum values at one frequency, usually a low hum. What matters is how wide the effect is and whether it works on voices. The order of quality in Nepal's market, roughly, is Sony and Apple and Bose at the top, Samsung, Soundcore Liberty, Redmi Pro and CMF Pro in the middle, OnePlus Nord, Realme and Soundcore P series below, and most Rs 2,000 pairs at the bottom.</p>
<h2>Battery: the number to look for</h2>
<p>Hours per charge with ANC on. Five is the minimum, seven is comfortable, ten is excellent. The case total tells you how often you need a socket. Fast charging saves you on the mornings you forgot. Batteries shrink by perhaps a fifth each year of heavy use, so start with more than you need.</p>
<h2>Calls</h2>
<p>Stems bring microphones closer to your mouth, so stemmed buds usually sound clearer than round ones. Wind is the enemy. Bone-conduction sensors help in premium pairs. Ask a friend to judge, from the footpath outside the shop.</p>
<h2>Codecs without the jargon</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>SBC:</strong> works everywhere, fine for most.</li>
<li><strong>AAC:</strong> best for iPhones.</li>
<li><strong>LDAC:</strong> high quality on many Androids, uses more battery.</li>
<li><strong>aptX family:</strong> on phones with Qualcomm chips that support it.</li>
<li><strong>LC3 and LE Audio:</strong> new, better calls and lower power, when both devices support it.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you stream music on a normal plan while walking, the codec is not your bottleneck.</p>
<h2>Multipoint, find my and other comforts</h2>
<p>Multipoint keeps two devices connected. In-ear detection pauses when you remove a bud. Find my buds plays a sound. Wireless charging is pleasant. An app with an equaliser and firmware updates is essential. Spatial audio is fun for films. Live translation needs the internet and patience.</p>
<h2>Water and dust</h2>
<p>IPX4 covers sweat and drizzle. IP54 and IP55 add dust. IPX7 survives a drop in water. The case usually has less protection. Dry the buds before you put them back.</p>
<h2>Which brand for which phone?</h2>
<p><strong>iPhone:</strong> AirPods for switching and find my, anything with AAC otherwise.</p>
<p><strong>Samsung:</strong> Galaxy Buds for the codec and switching between Galaxy devices.</p>
<p><strong>OnePlus, OPPO, Realme:</strong> their own buds share features.</p>
<p><strong>Xiaomi, POCO:</strong> Redmi Buds.</p>
<p><strong>Nothing, CMF:</strong> CMF Buds, with controls in the phone's settings.</p>
<p><strong>Any phone:</strong> Soundcore, Sony, JBL.</p>
<h2>Copies and how to avoid them</h2>
<p>Fake AirPods are sold across the country for Rs 1,500 to 4,000, and some are made well enough to show the pairing animation. Check the serial number on Apple's coverage page, look for the exact name in Bluetooth settings, and test noise cancelling. For JBL, Sony and Samsung, open the official app: it will not recognise a copy. A bill from an authorised shop is the best protection.</p>
<h2>Warranty in practice</h2>
<p>Most brands offer six to twelve months. Ask whether a faulty bud is replaced individually, and how long it takes. Lost buds are not covered. For expensive pairs, ask the price of a single bud and a case before you buy.</p>
<h2>How long will they last?</h2>
<p>Two to three years of daily use. The battery goes first. Clean the mesh monthly with a dry brush, wipe the tips with a damp cloth, keep the case out of the sun, and charge it every week or two even when idle.</p>
