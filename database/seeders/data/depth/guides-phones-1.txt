<h2>What Rs 15,000 really pays for</h2>
<p>After customs, VAT, the distributor's margin and the shop's margin, very little of your Rs 15,000 is left for the phone itself. Makers therefore choose one of three things to do properly, and save on the rest. Some give you a smooth screen. Some give you storage. Some give you a battery that lasts. Nobody gives you all three. If you know which one you need, you will be happy. If you expect a small version of a Rs 40,000 phone, you will not.</p>
<h2>Memory first, everything else second</h2>
<p>Android 15 and 16 are heavier than the Android of five years ago. A 3GB phone spends its life closing apps to make room for others. You switch from Facebook to the camera and back, and Facebook starts again. With 4GB that still happens, only less. So the first rule is simple: do not buy a 3GB phone new in 2026 unless it will be used only for calls.</p>
<p>The second rule is about storage. The system takes 15 to 20GB. WhatsApp takes another 10GB in a few months if you belong to family groups that share videos every morning. On a 64GB phone you will be deleting things by next Dashain. On 128GB you have a year or two. A memory card helps with photos and videos, though apps still live on the internal storage.</p>
<h2>Looking at each phone again</h2>
<p><strong>Tecno Spark Go 2.</strong> The 120Hz screen makes it feel quicker than it is, and the chip is as good as you will find here. It comes in several sizes. The 3GB/64GB version at the bottom is the one shown in advertisements. The 4GB/128GB version costs about Rs 3,500 more and is a different experience. Tecno's phones come with extra apps, so spend ten minutes removing them.</p>
<p><strong>Infinix Smart 10.</strong> Very similar hardware from a sister brand, with a brighter screen in sunlight. It runs the Go edition of Android, built for small memory, which is sensible. The 8MP camera is for documents and daylight.</p>
<p><strong>Blackview Wave 10C.</strong> It has the largest screen of the group at 6.88 inches with a plain 60Hz refresh rate, and the Unisoc T310 is the slowest chip here. Choose it only for someone who wants large text and simple use.</p>
<p><strong>Ai+ Pulse.</strong> A newer name in the market. The T615 chip is a little stronger. Before buying, ask where it is serviced and whether parts exist in Nepal. A phone without a service centre is a disposable phone.</p>
<h2>Cameras: what to expect</h2>
<p>Outdoors, in good light, all of these take pictures that look fine on WhatsApp. Indoors, at night or with anything that moves, they struggle. The number of lenses on the back is decoration. Two or three of the circles on cheap phones are depth sensors or nothing at all. Judge by taking one photo of the inside of the shop and zooming in.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging</h2>
<p>Every phone here has about 5,000mAh, and slow chips with HD screens use little energy, so a day and a half is normal. Charging at 10 to 15W takes two and a half to three hours. That means charging at night. If your area has frequent cuts, a small power bank is more useful than a faster charger.</p>
<h2>Who should buy at this price</h2>
<ul>
<li>A parent or grandparent who calls, uses Messenger and watches bhajans or news on YouTube.</li>
<li>A child's first phone, with parental controls on.</li>
<li>A second phone for a shop, a delivery rider or a business SIM.</li>
<li>A temporary phone while saving for a better one.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who should wait two months</h2>
<p>If this will be your only phone, with banking, online classes, TikTok and games, the experience at Rs 15,000 will be frustrating within the year. Adding Rs 6,000 to 10,000 changes everything: more memory, faster charging, a better screen. See what that buys in our guides to phones <a href="/guides/best-phones-under-20000-nepal">under Rs 20,000</a> and <a href="/guides/best-phones-under-25000-nepal">under Rs 25,000</a>.</p>
<h2>What about a used phone?</h2>
<p>For Rs 15,000 the second-hand market offers two-year-old Redmi, Samsung and Realme phones that were Rs 25,000 to 30,000 new. A clean one is faster than anything on this page. The risks are a tired battery, a replaced screen, and an IMEI that is not registered. Check the last one first, with the method in <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">our guide to official phones</a>. Ask for the original bill, test every function, and keep Rs 2,000 aside for a new battery.</p>
<h2>Five things to do on the first day</h2>
<ul>
<li>Uninstall or disable the games and shopping apps you did not ask for.</li>
<li>Turn off automatic media download in WhatsApp.</li>
<li>Install Facebook Lite where it is available, or use Facebook in the browser.</li>
<li>Switch animations to 0.5x in developer options. It makes slow phones feel quicker.</li>
<li>Set up Find My Device and a screen lock.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Security on cheap phones</h2>
<p>Budget phones receive few Android updates, sometimes none, and security patches for a limited time. If you use mobile banking or a wallet on one, install apps only from the Play Store, keep the apps updated, never install an APK sent on chat, and do not root the phone. Set a separate PIN for your wallet. These habits protect you more than any antivirus.</p>
<h2>Our pick</h2>
<p>For most people with exactly Rs 15,000, we would take the <a href="/products/tecno-spark-go-2">Tecno Spark Go 2</a> in the 4GB/128GB version, if your shop has it at or under Rs 15,000. <p>Advertisements speak of "8GB RAM with extension". The extension borrows storage and treats it as memory. At this price it is 4GB, which is enough for one thing at a time.</p>

<h2>4G and calls</h2>
<p>All of these are 4G phones. On NTC and Ncell that is what you will be using anyway. More important is VoLTE, which carries calls over 4G with clearer sound and less dropping. Insert your SIM in the shop and check that the option exists in network settings and that it works for your operator. Some budget models support it on one operator and not the other.</p>
<h2>Screens in daylight</h2>
<p>Most screens here give between 450 and 740 nits. In Terai sunshine you will shade the phone with your hand. If you work outdoors, look at the display in the street before paying, and choose the brighter one over the smoother one.</p>
<h2>After-sales: a realistic view</h2>
<p>A cheap phone breaks in expensive ways. A screen can cost Rs 3,000 to 5,000, about a quarter of the phone's price. A tempered glass and a case for Rs 500 are the best accessories you can buy. <p><strong>Samsung</strong> has the widest service and the longest updates, with modest batteries and slower charging. <strong>Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO</strong> give the most hardware for your money, and software that needs some tidying. <strong>vivo and OPPO</strong> make handsome phones with flattering cameras and large dealer networks. <strong>Realme</strong> leans towards battery and charging. <strong>Honor</strong> makes slim, tough phones with huge batteries, and its service is expanding. <strong>OnePlus and Nothing</strong> have the cleanest software among the Chinese-owned makers and fewer service points. <strong>Infinix and Tecno</strong> are strongest under Rs 40,000. <strong>Apple</strong> offers longevity, video quality and resale, at a price.</p>
<p>Our brand guides have full price lists, for example <a href="/guides/samsung-phone-price-in-nepal">Samsung</a>, <a href="/guides/xiaomi-phone-price-in-nepal">Xiaomi</a>, <a href="/guides/vivo-phone-price-in-nepal">vivo</a> and <a href="/guides/apple-phone-price-in-nepal">Apple</a>.</p>
<h2>Match the phone to the person</h2>
<p><strong>For a parent:</strong> a big, bright screen, loud speaker, long battery and nearby service. Galaxy A07 or A17, vivo Y series.</p>
<p><strong>For a college student:</strong> a smooth screen, 8GB of memory and enough speed for games. Infinix Note 50s, Redmi Note 15 5G, POCO F7.</p>
<p><strong>For someone who sells online:</strong> a stabilised main camera and good front camera. Moto G96, vivo V series, Galaxy A37.</p>
<p><strong>For a traveller or guide:</strong> battery and toughness. Honor X9d, Redmi Note 17 Pro, Realme P4 Power.</p>
<p><strong>For a photographer:</strong> vivo X300 Pro, Xiaomi 17 Ultra, OPPO Find X9 Pro.</p>
<p><strong>For someone who never wants to think about it:</strong> iPhone 17, Galaxy S26, OnePlus 15R.</p>
<h2>Features that matter, ranked</h2>
<ol>
<li>Memory and storage. They decide how the phone feels in year three.</li>
<li>Battery. Above 6,000mAh, most people stop worrying.</li>
<li>Main camera with optical stabilisation.</li>
<li>Screen brightness, then smoothness.</li>
<li>Years of security updates.</li>
<li>Charging speed, from 33W upwards.</li>
<li>Water and dust protection.</li>
<li>5G, if your area is likely to get it.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Features that matter less than advertised</h2>
<p>Macro and depth cameras. Memory extension. Peak brightness in HDR. Ultra-fast charging above 80W. The number of AI tools. Benchmark scores. A processor two names higher than the one you can afford. None of these will change your Tuesday.</p>
<h2>When to buy</h2>
<p>Prices follow a calendar. Samsung launches its S series in the first months of the year and its foldables in July, and older models fall before those dates. iPhones change in September, with discounts on the previous series from October. Dashain and Tihar bring broad offers, described in <a href="/blog/dashain-tihar-tech-deals-how-to-spot-a-real-discount">our festival deals article</a>. In 2026, component costs pushed many prices upwards, so waiting is not always rewarded. If your phone is broken, buy now. If it works, wait for a sale.</p>
<h2>Buying safely</h2>
<p>Whichever phone you choose, three checks protect your money. The IMEI must be registered, the bill must carry it, and the warranty must belong to the brand's distributor in Nepal. Our guide to <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">checking an official phone</a> shows how in five minutes.</p>
<h2>New, used or refurbished?</h2>
<p>A one-year-old flagship often beats a new mid-range phone at the same price on camera and build, and loses on battery and warranty. If you can judge a used phone or know the owner, it is worth considering above Rs 50,000. Below that, new phones are so capable that the saving is rarely worth the risk. Refurbished phones sold by shops vary widely. Ask who refurbished it, what was replaced and how long the guarantee lasts.</p>
<h2>Keeping it longer</h2>
<p>The best way to save money on phones is to buy fewer of them. A case, a screen protector, a charge limit of 80 per cent at night, 20GB of free space and a battery replacement in the third year can stretch almost any phone here by a year or two. At Rs 4,000 to 8,000, a new battery is the best value upgrade in the whole market.</p>
