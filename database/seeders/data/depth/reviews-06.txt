<p>This is the first question to ask the shop. On some IdeaPad Slim 3 models the memory is soldered, and on others there is a slot. If yours has a slot, replacing or adding a stick to reach 8GB or more would change our opinion of the machine. Do not accept "probably". Ask the seller to check the model code in Lenovo's PSREF document, or open it in front of you.</p>
<h2>The TN screen</h2>
<p>The 15.6-inch Full HD panel uses the older TN type. Seen straight on, it is acceptable. Tilt it, and colours invert or fade. Two people cannot comfortably watch the same film. For spreadsheets and text it does the job.</p>
<h2>Storage</h2>
<p>With 128GB you face the same trouble we describe in our <a href="/reviews/asus-vivobook-14-x1404va-i3-review-nepal">Vivobook Core i3 review</a>: updates need room, and there is little to spare. An SSD upgrade is usually possible.</p>
<h2>Realistic uses</h2>
<ul>
<li>A reception desk running a single billing page.</li>
<li>A typing-practice machine for a training centre.</li>
<li>A kitchen or prayer-room screen for YouTube.</li>
<li>A light Linux computer for a hobbyist.</li>
</ul>
<p>With Linux Mint or ChromeOS Flex in place of Windows, 4GB goes further, and the laptop becomes quite usable for browsing, documents and video. That is an honest way to give it a good life, as long as you do not need Windows programs.</p>
<h2>Better ways to spend Rs 55,000</h2>
[[rivals:lenovo-ideapad-slim-3-15ian8-2023-intel-n100]]
<p>The list of new laptops at this price is short and weak. Think wider.</p>
<p><strong>A used business laptop.</strong> ThinkPad, Latitude and EliteBook models with a Core i5, 16GB and 256GB or more appear in the used market at around this price. They have better keyboards, screens and bodies. Have the battery and hinges checked and ask for at least a short shop warranty.</p>
<p><strong>A tablet with a keyboard.</strong> For reading, notes and online classes, an 11-inch tablet with a Bluetooth keyboard is quicker and lasts longer. See our <a href="/guides/best-tablets-under-50000-nepal">tablets under Rs 50,000</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Waiting two or three months.</strong> At Rs 70,000 to 75,000 you reach laptops with 8GB and 256GB or 512GB that will last through a degree. The <a href="/products/acer-aspire-lite-14-al14-45p-ryzen-3-5400u">Acer Aspire Lite 14</a> is one of them. EMI can bridge the gap if the total cost is fair.</p>
<p><strong>A desktop for home.</strong> If the computer will never move, a small desktop or a mini PC with a monitor gives far more power per rupee. Our <a href="/custom-pc-build">PC builder</a> can plan one.</p>
<h2>If you already bought one</h2>
<p>Do not despair. First, check for a memory slot and go to 8GB if you can. Second, install a 256GB or 512GB SSD. Third, remove everything that starts with Windows. Fourth, use a light browser such as Edge in efficiency mode with no more than five tabs. Fifth, use web versions of Office. Those steps can make it tolerable.</p>
<h2>Advice for schools and offices</h2>
<p>Cheap machines bought in bulk often cost more in the end, since staff time is lost and replacements come early. If you are buying ten computers for a lab, ask suppliers for refurbished business desktops with 8GB and SSDs. They are faster, easier to repair and frequently cheaper than new laptops of this class.</p>
<h2>Who should consider it despite everything</h2>
<p>Someone with a precise, tiny need and no other option, or someone who plans to run Linux. Everyone else should treat the Rs 54,999 sticker as a warning sign. The price looks like a bargain because the parts that make a laptop pleasant have been removed.</p>
<h2>The rule to remember</h2>
<p>For Windows in 2026: 8GB of memory is the floor, 16GB is comfortable, 256GB of storage is the floor, and an IPS screen is worth asking for. If a new laptop misses two of those, as this one misses three, leave it in the shop.</p>

<h2>The case for a quiet premium laptop</h2>
<p>Between the budget machines at one lakh and the showpieces at two and a half there is a gap that few laptops fill well. The Yoga Slim 7i Aura Edition does. For lawyers, accountants, researchers, managers and postgraduate students, that combination is more valuable than a graphics card.</p>
<h2>Lunar Lake, explained simply</h2>
<p>Intel designed the Core Ultra 200V chips around efficiency. Memory sits on the same package as the processor, background tasks run on low-power cores, and standby uses very little. The result is a Windows laptop that behaves more like a MacBook: it wakes quickly, stays cool on your lap and keeps its charge overnight. The Core Ultra 5 226V is the entry model, and for office work you will not feel any lack.</p>
<p>Two limits follow from the design. The memory cannot be changed, so 16GB is what you have for life. And heavy multi-core work, such as compiling a large code base or rendering, is slower than on H-series chips that use more power. Choose according to your work.</p>
<h2>The OLED screen</h2>
<p>A 14-inch panel with 600 nits and full DCI-P3 colour is far above the norm. Reading is comfortable, photographs look right, and films are a pleasure. Editors of pictures and short videos can trust the colours. The 16:10 shape shows more of each page.</p>
<p>As with every OLED, use dark mode, hide the taskbar and let the pixel care run, to avoid uneven wear after some years.</p>
<h2>Battery life in practice</h2>
<p>With moderate brightness and typical office tools, a full working day away from a socket is realistic, and light use goes further. That is an advantage in a city with power cuts, and for people who travel between meetings. It charges by USB-C, so one charger can serve your phone as well.</p>
<h2>Webcam, sound and keyboard</h2>
<p>Lenovo's premium models have sharp webcams with a physical shutter, quality microphones and speakers that sound fuller than you expect from a thin machine. <h2>Gaming and creative work</h2>
<p>The integrated Arc 130V graphics can run older and lighter games at reduced settings. It can edit 4K clips on a short timeline. It is not for modern games or 3D work.
