=== samsung-galaxy-a57-5g-review-nepal
<h2>The full sheet</h2>
[[specs:samsung-galaxy-a57-5g]]
<h2>Why so many people end up with this phone</h2>
<p>Walk into a mobile shop in Butwal or Dharan and say you have about a lakh and want something reliable. The A57 is very likely the first box that comes out. Some of that is habit, and some of it is fair. Samsung has authorised service in more Nepali cities than any other Android brand, spare parts are stocked, and a shop can tell you the cost of a screen before you break it. A phone is a four-year purchase for most families, and support is part of what you are buying.</p>
<p>The design helps as well. At 6.9mm and about 179 grams it feels closer to a flagship than to a mid-ranger. Many rivals with huge batteries weigh 210 grams or more. If you have ever owned a heavy phone, you know the difference on a long scooter ride with it in a front pocket.</p>
<h2>Screen</h2>
<p>The 6.7-inch Super AMOLED is the part you will enjoy most. Colours are rich without being silly in the Natural mode, black is truly black for night reading, and 120Hz keeps scrolling steady. The glass is Gorilla Glass Victus+, which resists drops well. There is nothing exotic here, such as the 144Hz or 1.5K panels you find on Chinese phones, and nobody who is not comparing two phones next to each other will miss them.</p>
<h2>Performance over time</h2>
<p>The Exynos 1680 is a 4nm chip in the upper middle of the market. It handles Facebook, banking, Google Meet and ordinary photo editing without hesitation. Games such as Mobile Legends and Free Fire run well. Genshin Impact and similar titles need reduced settings.</p>
<p>What matters more is how it behaves in year three. Samsung's One UI has become lighter, and 8GB of memory is still comfortable for this class. If you plan to use all six years of updates, the 12GB version for Rs 11,000 more is easier to live with at the end. If you change phones every three years, save the money.</p>
<h2>Camera: good where it counts</h2>
<p>The 50MP main camera is dependable. Daylight shots are sharp, faces look natural, and night mode brightens a street without turning it orange. The 12MP ultra wide is above average for the class, since most competitors use 8MP. It also has enough detail for group photos indoors. The 5MP macro is there to fill a space on the back.</p>
<p>There is no telephoto. Two-times zoom, which crops the main sensor, is fine. Beyond that, detail goes quickly. A parent photographing a school programme from the back row will be happier with the <a href="/products/nothing-phone-4a">Nothing Phone 4a</a>.</p>
<p>The 12MP front camera sounds modest beside 32MP rivals. In practice it produces pleasing skin and handles backlight well, which counts for more on video calls.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging in a normal week</h2>
<p>Five thousand milliamp-hours gets most people to bedtime. Heavy users on mobile data will need a top-up. The 45W charging refills about two-thirds of the battery in half an hour with a suitable charger, which is not supplied. Wireless charging is not available. If your house loses power for hours, rivals with 7,000mAh or more give you a wider safety margin, and no software feature makes up for that.</p>
<h2>Six years of updates in plain terms</h2>
<p>The phone launches with Android 16, and Samsung promises six generations of upgrades and six years of security patches. Banking apps and digital wallets drop old Android versions over time, so this directly affects how long the phone stays useful. It also affects resale. A two-year-old Galaxy A with four years of support left is easy to sell, while a similar phone from a brand with two years of support is not.</p>
<h2>Price: the uncomfortable part</h2>
<p>At Rs 96,999 the A57 costs nearly as much as phones with flagship chips.</p>
[[rivals:samsung-galaxy-a57-5g]]
<p>The <a href="/products/oneplus-15r">OnePlus 15R</a> and <a href="/products/poco-x8-pro-max">POCO X8 Pro Max</a> are much faster. The <a href="/products/xiaomi-17t">Xiaomi 17T</a> has a stronger camera set. The <a href="/products/oneplus-nord-6">OnePlus Nord 6</a> is cheaper and lasts far longer on a charge. Each of them gives up something the Samsung has, usually years of updates, service reach or slimness. You have to decide what those are worth to you, and it is perfectly reasonable to decide that they are worth it.</p>
<p>There is also competition from inside the family. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a37-5g">Galaxy A37 5G</a> costs around Rs 22,000 less, and shares the same software approach. If your use is light, the A37 is the smarter Samsung. And during festival sales, last year's Galaxy S models sometimes fall close to the A57's price, at which point they are the better phone.</p>
<h2>Who it fits</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Parents and relatives</strong> who want something familiar, light and supported for years.</li>
<li><strong>Office users</strong> who live in email, calls and documents and value local service.</li>
<li><strong>People in smaller cities</strong> where Samsung is the only brand with a nearby service centre.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who should look elsewhere</h2>
<ul>
<li>Gamers, who get far more frames per rupee from POCO or OnePlus.</li>
<li>Travellers and delivery workers, who need a bigger battery.</li>
<li>Anyone who wants zoom.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Buying advice</h2>
<p>Wait for an offer if you can. Samsung Nepal runs exchange bonuses and EMI schemes for most of the year, and the sticker price is rarely the final one. Ask for the official warranty sticker, check the IMEI in MDMS and confirm the memory version on the box, because the 8GB and 12GB models look identical. Finally, buy a charger from a known brand with PPS support, or you will pay for 45W charging and receive 15W.</p>

<p>The A17 5G is bought by three groups of people more than any others: students getting their first decent phone, sons and daughters buying for their parents, and people who need a second number that will not let them down. <p>Samsung's Easy mode deserves a mention here. It makes icons and text larger, simplifies the keyboard and lengthens the time needed for a long press. Set that up, pin WhatsApp, the camera and the dialler to the home screen, and an older user can manage without lessons.</p>
<h2>A proper screen for the money</h2>
<p>Super AMOLED at Full HD+ is still rare at this price. Text is sharp, contrast is strong and 1,100 nits is bright enough for reading outdoors in shade. The glass is Gorilla Glass Victus, an unusually tough choice for a budget phone and a good one for people who drop things.</p>
<p>The refresh rate is 90Hz. Phones at the same price offer 120Hz, and if you place the two next to each other you can see it while scrolling fast. On its own, 90Hz looks smooth. The notch at the top is the dated U shape, so the front of the phone will not impress anybody.</p>
<h2>What the Exynos 1330 can and cannot do</h2>
<p>This is a modest processor. It opens WhatsApp, Facebook, YouTube and eSewa without drama, keeps a handful of apps in memory, and plays casual games. Free Fire works at standard settings. <p>For that reason, spend the extra Rs 4,000 on 8GB of memory if you can. On this class of chip, memory is the difference between a phone that feels acceptable in 2028 and one that does not. The 8GB/256GB version at Rs 56,999 is harder to justify, since it costs as much as clearly faster phones. The microSD slot is the cheaper answer to storage.</p>
<h2>Cameras with one real advantage</h2>

<p>The 5MP ultra wide is for daylight only, and you can ignore the 2MP macro. The 13MP selfie camera is fine for video calls.</p>
<h2>Battery, charging and the missing charger</h2>
<p>A 5,000mAh battery with an undemanding chip lasts a full day, and nearer a day and a half for light users. Charging at 25W is slow by current standards, taking about an hour and a half from empty, and the charger is usually not in the box. For someone who plugs in overnight, none of this matters.</p>
<p>IP54 means dust and splashes. Light rain on the way to the bus is fine. Dropping it in a bucket is not.</p>
<h2>5G in Nepal: is it worth paying for?</h2>

If you would never move off 4G, the 4G-only phones at this price sometimes have bigger batteries or faster charging.</p>
<h2>Updates are the hidden feature</h2>
<p>Samsung has been giving its cheaper A-series phones a long run of updates, far longer than most rivals at this price. For a phone that will be handed down, that keeps banking and wallet apps working, and it keeps the owner safer from malware. <h2>Specifications at a glance</h2>

<h2>How 120Hz changes the base iPhone</h2>
<p>For four years people compared a Rs 1.5 lakh iPhone with a Rs 40,000 Android and asked why the cheaper phone scrolled more smoothly. That conversation is over. The iPhone 17 uses the same kind of variable panel as the Pro, running from a very low refresh rate on a still page up to 120Hz when you scroll. It also makes an always-on lock screen possible, so the time and your widgets are visible on the desk.</p>
<p>Go from an iPhone 13 or 14 to this and the screen is the first thing you will notice. Text stays readable while it moves, animations feel lighter, and the 3,000-nit peak makes maps usable at noon. If you already have a Pro from the last few years, you will see no difference.</p>
<h2>The cameras in daily life</h2>
<p>There are two lenses on the back, both 48MP. The main one covers ninety per cent of what people shoot: family, food, streets, documents, pets. It also provides a 2x setting by cropping the centre of the sensor, which is good enough for portraits and half-length shots. The ultra wide now has the resolution to capture a whole room or a mountain view with detail in the corners, and it works for close-ups of flowers or jewellery.</p>
<p>What you do not get is reach. At 5x or 10x the phone is enlarging pixels. At a football match at Dasharath Stadium, or with birds across a lake, the Pro models and many Android phones will beat it easily. Think back over the last hundred photos on your current phone. If fewer than five were zoomed, you will not miss the lens.</p>
<p>The front camera is the surprise of this generation. It has a square sensor, so you can hold the phone upright and still take a wide group selfie. When more people step into the frame, it widens on its own. For creators who film themselves, this is more useful than any change on the back.</p>
<h2>Speed, memory and Apple Intelligence</h2>
<p>The A19 is quicker than the chips in almost every Android phone at the same price, and the interface never hesitates. The 8GB of memory is the minimum for Apple Intelligence. It is enough today, and it is the one figure that could feel tight towards the end of the phone's life if on-device AI features grow. Apple usually supports an iPhone for six years or more, so this is a minor doubt and not a reason to avoid it.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging</h2>
<p>Apple quotes a full day, and in mixed use that is realistic. Bright sun, hotspot use and mobile games will shorten it. The welcome change is charging speed: about half full in twenty minutes with a 40W-class USB-C charger. MagSafe at up to 25W is quick for a wireless system. There is no charger in the box, so plan for one, and buy a reputable brand. A fake charger is the cheapest way to damage an expensive phone.</p>
<h2>Is the 512GB model worth it?</h2>
<p>No, for most people. The jump from Rs 1,64,999 to Rs 2,15,699 is more than Rs 50,000 for 256GB of storage. At that point you are within sight of the <a href="/products/apple-iphone-17-pro">iPhone 17 Pro</a>. Since the base model now starts at 256GB, it is enough for anyone who backs photos up to iCloud or a computer. Pay for an iCloud plan or buy an external SSD, which plugs straight into the USB-C port.</p>
<h2>Cost of ownership</h2>
<p>An iPhone costs more at the counter and gives more back later. Two years from now a clean iPhone 17 with its box and bill should sell for a larger share of its price than an Android phone bought on the same day. Repairs are the other side. Screen and back-glass replacements with genuine parts are expensive, so use a case, and consider what you would do if you cracked the screen in the first month.</p>
<p>Official warranty matters too. Apple products sold through authorised resellers in Nepal carry a one-year warranty handled by authorised service providers in Kathmandu. Outside the valley, expect to send the phone.</p>
<h2>Compared with phones at about this price</h2>
[[rivals:iphone-17]]
<p>The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s26">Galaxy S26</a> is the natural rival. It is Rs 25,000 cheaper, similar in size, and has a telephoto lens and seven years of updates. If you have no attachment to iOS, it is hard to argue against. The <a href="/products/oneplus-15">OnePlus 15</a> costs Rs 25,000 more and offers a battery that lasts almost twice as long. The <a href="/products/google-pixel-10-pro">Pixel 10 Pro</a> takes wonderful photos, but has no official support here.</p>
<p>People choose the iPhone for reasons outside the spec sheet: iMessage and FaceTime with relatives abroad, AirDrop with a MacBook, an Apple Watch, steady app quality, and resale. If three or more of those apply to you, the comparison ends there.</p>
<h2>Upgrading from an older iPhone</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>From an iPhone 11 or 12:</strong> a huge step in every respect. Do it.</li>
<li><strong>From a 13 or 14:</strong> worthwhile for the screen, camera and USB-C, if your battery is tired anyway.</li>
<li><strong>From a 15 or 16:</strong> wait. The difference is mainly the 120Hz display.</li>
<li><strong>From any Pro since the 14:</strong> you would lose a lens. Skip it.</li>

<p>Most flagships are designed around a thin body, and the battery gets whatever space is left. OnePlus did it the other way round. <p>If you are a lighter user, you can charge every second day. That has a hidden benefit. Batteries age by the number of full cycles, and a phone that needs half as many will hold its health for longer.</p>
<p>Charging is fast in both forms: up to 120W with a compatible OnePlus SUPERVOOC charger and 50W on the matching wireless stand. With an ordinary USB PD charger it is slower. Ask the shop which charger is in the box, or whether one is included at all, as listings in Nepal do not agree.</p>
<h2>Speed, and how long it lasts</h2>
<p>The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 is in the top group of phone chips, and paired with 16GB of fast memory and UFS 4.1 storage it makes everything feel immediate. The interesting question with any such chip is heat. A large phone with a large vapour chamber can keep its performance up longer than a compact one, which is one reason gamers like OnePlus. The 165Hz mode works in a list of supported titles and drops back to 120Hz elsewhere.</p>
<p>In a hot room without a fan, no phone holds its peak speed for an hour. Take off the case, lower the brightness and avoid charging while you play. Those steps matter more than the chip's name.</p>
<h2>Screen</h2>
<p>The 6.78-inch AMOLED adapts between 1Hz and 120Hz in normal use. It is flat, which makes it easy to fit a protector and reduces accidental touches. The <a href="/products/xiaomi-17-ultra">Xiaomi 17 Ultra</a> gathers more light with its one-inch sensor, and the <a href="/products/vivo-x300-pro">vivo X300 Pro</a> has far more detail at long zoom. === xiaomi-17-ultra-review-nepal
<h2>Specifications</h2>
[[specs:xiaomi-17-ultra]]
<h2>Why sensor size is the whole story</h2>
<p>Phone adverts talk about megapixels because large numbers are easy to print. Photographers look at the size of the sensor. A one-inch-type sensor has several times the area of the one in a mid-range phone, so it collects several times as much light in the same fraction of a second. That gives you three things: less grain in the dark, faster shutter speeds for moving children, and a background that blurs because of the lens and not because software guessed where the edges were.</p>
<p>LOFIC, the technology Xiaomi mentions, is about contrast. Each pixel can store more light before it clips to white, so a bright window behind a person, or the sky above a shaded courtyard, keeps its detail in a single exposure. For anyone who photographs the mountains at sunrise, with dark foreground and blazing snow, this solves a real problem.</p>
<h2>The zoom that moves</h2>
<p>Most phones have fixed zoom lenses and crop between them. The 17 Ultra's 200MP periscope physically changes focal length between 3.2x and 4.3x. Inside that range you lose no quality as you frame a portrait or a detail, and the high resolution leaves room to crop well beyond it. It is the closest thing to a real lens on a phone.</p>
<p>For long distances the <a href="/products/vivo-x300-pro">vivo X300 Pro</a> remains a strong competitor with its 200MP 3.7x camera. The two phones cost the same, so the choice is about style. Xiaomi is the better everyday and low-light camera. vivo is the better choice for the far end of the zoom and for stage photography.</p>
<h2>Taking photos with it</h2>
<p>You pick between two Leica looks. Authentic keeps shadows and restrained colour, Vibrant is closer to what other phones produce. A professional mode gives manual control over shutter, focus and white balance, and you can save RAW files for editing. People who already own a mirrorless camera often find that this phone replaces it for travel, while people who only want to point and shoot will be paying for a lot of capability that stays in the menus.</p>
<p>Video has improved, with stable 4K and flat colour profiles for grading. The iPhone still leads for effortless, consistent clips, particularly inside apps such as Instagram and TikTok, whose cameras are usually tuned better for Apple and Samsung.</p>
<h2>Everything else is flagship too</h2>
<p>The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 and 16GB of memory mean there is no task this phone struggles with. The 6.9-inch OLED is bright, accurate and adapts from 1Hz to 120Hz. A 6,000mAh battery is large for a camera phone and comfortably covers a day of shooting. It charges at 90W by cable and 50W wirelessly, and can send 22.5W to another device, enough to properly charge a small camera or earbuds.</p>
<p>Protection is rated IP66, IP68 and IP69. The camera island is enormous, though, and it makes the phone top-heavy. Use a strap or a grip case. A drop onto that lens ring would be an expensive afternoon.</p>
<h2>HyperOS and the things to change first</h2>
<p>HyperOS 3 is fast and full of features. Spend a little time on it: uninstall the extra apps, turn off recommendations in the built-in tools, allow WhatsApp and Viber to run in the background, and set the camera to open with a double press of the volume key. Xiaomi's update promise for its top phones has grown, yet it remains shorter than Samsung's seven years.</p>
<h2>Is this a good idea in Nepal?</h2>
<p>There are practical points to consider. Xiaomi has official service here, but the Ultra is a low-volume model, and parts such as its camera module may need ordering. Ask how long a screen replacement would take. Resale is the second point: Android camera phones lose value faster than iPhones and Galaxy Ultras. If you buy it to keep and to photograph with, that does not matter. If you sell every year, it does.</p>
<p>Third, check the version. Chinese units are common in the grey market and cost less, but they bring Chinese software, fewer Google conveniences and no local warranty. The official Nepali unit at Rs 1,99,999 is the one we price.</p>
<h2>Compared with others at this price</h2>
[[rivals:xiaomi-17-ultra]]
<p>The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s26-ultra">Galaxy S26 Ultra</a> does more non-camera things: a stylus, a privacy screen, DeX and long support. The <a href="/products/oneplus-15">OnePlus 15</a> lasts much longer on a charge. The <a href="/products/google-pixel-10-pro-xl">Pixel 10 Pro XL</a> has the most reliable point-and-shoot results, with no official service. Only the Xiaomi and the vivo feel like cameras that happen to make calls.</p>
<h2>A question to ask yourself</h2>
<p>Open the photo gallery on your current phone and look at the last month. Are there portraits, night scenes, travel and details that you wish were better? Then this phone will give you pleasure every week. Are they mostly screenshots, documents and food? Then the same money would be better spent on a phone with a longer battery, or half of it on something sensible and the other half on a trip worth photographing.</p>

<li>People who enjoy editing RAW files.</li>
<li>Owners of older Leica-tuned Xiaomi phones who want a clear step up.</li>

<h2>Who pays Rs 64,999 for earbuds?</h2>
<p>At this price you could buy a decent phone, so it is fair to start with the reason anyone would. The people who get value from the XM6 share a pattern: they wear earbuds for three or more hours a day, in noisy places, and they care about either silence or sound quality. A consultant who flies weekly, a student who lives beside Ring Road and needs to study, a developer in an open office, a musician who travels. For them the cost spreads over a thousand hours of use a year. For someone who listens for twenty minutes at the gym, it is a poor purchase.</p>
<h2>Noise cancelling: what is possible</h2>
<p>Sony's approach is to use several microphones on each bud with a dedicated processor, and to adapt to the noise around you and the seal in your ear. It is at its best with constant low sound: bus and aircraft engines, air conditioners, generators, fans. It reduces chatter noticeably. It softens horns and barking, and cannot remove them, since they are too sudden.</p>
<p>The seal decides how good it gets. Try every tip in the box, use the fit test in the app, and repeat it after a week as the foam settles. If one ear feels less isolated, try a different size on that side. Many people have ears that do not match.</p>
<p>Please do not use noise cancelling while riding, driving or crossing roads.</p>
<h2>Sound</h2>
<p>The XM line is tuned for a full, easy sound with deep bass and clear vocals. The app's equaliser can make it neutral in a minute. On Android you can use LDAC for higher-bitrate streaming, or LC3, the newer efficient codec, on phones that support Bluetooth LE Audio. On an iPhone you get AAC. The difference between LDAC and AAC is real but modest, and you need well-mastered music from a high-quality service to hear it. Free-tier streams will not reveal it.</p>
<h2>Size and comfort</h2>
<p>Each bud weighs 6.2 grams, a little more than the XM5's 5.9 grams, and the body is larger than an AirPod's. People with small ears should try them first. Foam tips are comfortable for long sessions, though they get dirty and need replacing every few months. Spare tips in Sony's sizes can be hard to find locally, so it is wise to buy a set when you see one.</p>
<h2>Calls and multipoint</h2>
<p>For people who spend their day in meetings, call quality may matter more than music. The microphones and voice pickup do a good job indoors and in moderate noise. Multipoint keeps a phone and a laptop connected together, so a call on one interrupts a video on the other without re-pairing. It is reliable once set up, though switching on LDAC may limit it depending on the phone.</p>
<h2>Battery</h2>
<p>Up to 24 hours in total with the case is enough for a working week of commuting. A short charge gives an hour or so of listening, and the case supports wireless charging. As with all earbuds, the cells inside are tiny and will lose capacity in two to three years of daily use. Treat that as part of the price.</p>
<h2>Water resistance</h2>
<p>IPX4 covers sweat and splashes. It is enough for walking in drizzle and light exercise. For running in the monsoon or hard gym sessions, choose a sports model with IP55 or better and hooks that hold it in place.</p>
<h2>XM6, XM5 or something else?</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/sony-wf-1000xm5">WF-1000XM5</a> is about Rs 15,000 cheaper and remains excellent. The newer model improves noise cancelling, call pickup and the connection. If you already own the XM5, keep it until the battery fades. If you are buying your first pair of premium Sony earbuds, the XM6 will stay current for longer, but the older pair is better value whenever the gap is large.</p>
<p>For iPhone owners the <a href="/products/airpods-pro-3">AirPods Pro 3</a> at Rs 47,999 are the sensible alternative. They pair instantly with every Apple device, offer personalised spatial audio and cost Rs 17,000 less. On Android they lose most of those benefits.</p>
<p>If you want quiet without the luxury price, the <a href="/products/soundcore-liberty-5-pro">Soundcore Liberty 5 Pro</a> and similar models give you strong noise cancelling and a good app. Our <a href="/guides/best-earbuds-under-20000-nepal">guide to earbuds under Rs 20,000</a> goes lower still. And if comfort and battery matter more than pocket size, full-size headphones such as Sony's WH series cancel more noise for similar money.</p>

<ul>
<li>Run the ear-tip fit test.</li>
<li>Turn off the feature that pauses music when you talk, unless you like it.</li>
<li>Limit maximum volume. Noise cancelling means you can listen more quietly, which protects your hearing.</li>

<h2>Buying genuine ones</h2>
<p>Counterfeit Sony earbuds are common and increasingly convincing. Buy from a seller who issues a VAT bill and an official warranty. Pair them with Sony's app in the shop: a genuine set is recognised, shows its serial and firmware and completes the fit test. Check that the serial number on the case matches the box. If the price is far below Rs 64,999, be suspicious.</p>
<h2>Care</h2>
<p>Wipe the tips and charging contacts weekly, dry them after sweat, and keep the case away from keys and coins. Do not leave them in a car in summer, since heat is the fastest way to age the batteries. With that small amount of care, they should give you several good years.</p>
