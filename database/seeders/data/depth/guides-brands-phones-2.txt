<p>Infinix belongs to Transsion, the company behind Tecno and itel. It sells huge numbers of phones in Africa and South Asia and has learned to make a small budget go far. <h2>Four families</h2>
<p><strong>Smart</strong> is entry level. <strong>Hot</strong> is the young, affordable line. <strong>Note</strong> is the one with the best hardware. <strong>GT</strong> is for games, with a bold back and sometimes lights. <h2>Smart 10 and 10 Plus</h2>
<p>At Rs 15,000 to 16,000 these are basic phones done properly. The <a href="/products/infinix-smart-10-plus">10 Plus</a> is the better buy, with 128GB and 6,000mAh. <h2>Note 50x: the bargain</h2>
<p>At about Rs 21,999 the <a href="/products/infinix-note-50x">Note 50x</a> has a Dimensity 7300 Ultimate, the same chip class you find in Rs 45,000 phones. <h2>Hot 50 Pro Plus: the screen</h2>
<p>An AMOLED at 120Hz with 8GB for Rs 25,999. If films matter more than games, choose it over the Note 50x. The chip is fine for daily use.</p>
<h2>Note 50s: the complete one</h2>
<p>For Rs 35,999 you get speed, a 144Hz AMOLED, fast memory and storage, and a stabilised 64MP camera. It is our general recommendation between Rs 35,000 and 40,000. See how it compares in <a href="/guides/best-phones-under-40000-nepal">phones under Rs 40,000</a>.</p>

<li>Switch off lock-screen content.</li>
<li>Keep the useful ones: game mode, social turbo, the IR remote, and XArena.</li>
<li>Allow your chat apps to run in the background.</li>

<h2>Motorola's place in Nepal</h2>
<p>Motorola, owned by Lenovo, has a small but loyal following here. People come for the software, which is close to Google's, and for designs with soft vegan-leather backs and curved screens that feel dearer than they are. The official range is short. Many more models arrive from India, and that is where care is needed.</p>
<h2>G or Edge?</h2>
<p><strong>Moto G</strong> phones cover Rs 30,000 to 45,000. "Power" means a larger battery. Two-digit numbers show rank and not age: the G96 is the best G and the G06 the simplest. <strong>Edge</strong> phones are slim and more expensive, and "Fusion" is the affordable Edge.</p>
<h2>G35 5G and G06 Power</h2>
<p>Both hover around Rs 31,000 and neither is easy to recommend. The G06 Power has a 7,000mAh battery with a basic chip. The G35 has 5G, 5,000mAh and 18W charging. At the same price a Redmi 15 4G, Spark 40 Pro or Redmi 17 5G gives more. Choose them only for the clean software.</p>
<h2>G57 Power: the sensible one</h2>
<p>For Rs 37,499 the <a href="/products/moto-g57-power">G57 Power</a> offers a modern Snapdragon 6s Gen 4, 7,000mAh, 8GB and Android 16. Its screen is an LCD and its charger is not in the box. It is the best Motorola for value, and it appears in our <a href="/guides/best-phones-under-40000-nepal">phones under Rs 40,000</a>.</p>
<h2>G96: the beautiful one</h2>
<p>A curved 144Hz pOLED, a light body and a stabilised Sony camera at Rs 44,999. <a href="/reviews/moto-g96-review-nepal">Our review</a> explains why we enjoy it and why people who travel all day should not buy it.</p>
<h2>Edge 50 Fusion: the older bargain</h2>
<p>At Rs 46,499 the <a href="/products/motorola-edge-50-fusion">Edge 50 Fusion</a> shares the chip of the G96 and adds 68W charging and IP68. It is older, so ask about remaining updates. If you find it at Rs 40,000, it is excellent.</p>
<h2>Edge 70 Fusion</h2>
<p>At Rs 79,999 it moves to a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4, 12GB and 7,000mAh in a slim body. It competes with the OnePlus Nord 6, Honor 600 and POCO X8 Pro Max, which are faster or better with photos. Its attraction is elegance and endurance together. Look for an offer.</p>
<h2>The software experience</h2>
<p>Motorola phones feel light. The launcher is simple, notifications are respected, and useful gestures are built in: chop twice for the torch, twist for the camera, lift to unlock. Smart Connect links the phone with a PC, tablet or TV and is better than most similar tools. There are a few preinstalled apps and a lock-screen feed on cheaper models that you can turn off.</p>
<h2>The update problem</h2>
<p>Here is the weak spot. Many G phones get one or two Android versions and three to four years of security patches, and they arrive late. Edge models do better. If you keep a phone five years or use it for sensitive work, Samsung and others are safer. Read the promise for your model on Motorola's site.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging</h2>
<p>Power models last two days. Others are ordinary. Charging ranges from 18 to 68W. Motorola uses USB PD widely, so common chargers work. Boxes in Nepal do not always include one.</p>
<h2>Cameras</h2>
<p>Motorola's cameras have improved, with Sony sensors and stabilisation from the G96 upwards. Colour is natural, portraits are decent, and nights are average. The front cameras are sharp and good for video calls.</p>
<h2>Durability</h2>
<p>Vegan leather does not show fingerprints or crack, and it gives grip. Curved glass is fragile at the edge. Several models carry IP64 to IP68 ratings and some pass military tests, but ratings differ by region, so check the exact model.</p>
<h2>Service in Nepal</h2>
<p>This is the question to ask first. Official service is limited, mostly to Kathmandu. A curved pOLED can take weeks to arrive and cost a third of the phone. Outside the valley, choose a brand with a nearby centre, or accept the risk and use a good case.</p>
<h2>Indian imports</h2>
<p>Because Motorola sells a great variety online in India, border shops carry models that have no official presence here. They are cheap and tempting. There is no Nepali warranty, the IMEI needs to be registered, and returns are difficult. If you buy one, verify MDMS before paying, and check that the charger and bill are included.</p>
<h2>Resale</h2>
<p>Low. Few buyers look for a used Motorola. Keep it for three years or more, or buy it already discounted.</p>
<h2>How Motorola compares</h2>

<p><strong>With Samsung:</strong> Motorola gives nicer hardware and Samsung gives years and service.</p>
<p><strong>With Redmi:</strong> Redmi gives more hardware and clutter.</p>
<p><strong>With OnePlus:</strong> OnePlus is faster and dearer.</p>
<h2>Who should buy a Motorola</h2>
<ul>
<li>People who value simple software and a light phone.</li>
<li>Those who live in Kathmandu and take care of their things.</li>
<li>Users who connect the phone to a PC or TV.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who should not</h2>
<ul>
<li>Buyers who need service in other cities.</li>
<li>Long-term owners.</li>
<li>Gamers.</li>

<h2>Pixels in Nepal: start with the problem</h2>
<p>Google does not sell Pixel phones officially in Nepal. Every unit here comes through an importer, from India, the Gulf, the United States, Europe or Japan. That affects warranty, network features, MDMS registration and repairs. If you accept it, you get some of the best phones made. If you need a service counter in your city, choose something else and save yourself trouble.</p>
<h2>Why people still want them</h2>
<p><strong>The camera.</strong> Pixels take reliable photos with natural skin and controlled highlights. You press the button once, and it is right.</p>
<p><strong>The software.</strong> Pure Android, the first to receive new versions, with call screening, voice recorder with transcripts, Now Playing and thoughtful details.</p>
<p><strong>The support.</strong> Seven years of Android and security updates on recent models.</p>
<p><strong>The size.</strong> At 6.3 inches, the 10a, 10 and 10 Pro suit a hand.</p>
<h2>The models</h2>
<p><strong>Pixel 10a, about Rs 86,000.</strong> The sensible one. The Tensor G4, a 5,100mAh battery, a very good main camera, and the same seven years. It lacks the telephoto and some AI features.</p>
<p><strong>Pixel 10, about Rs 1,28,000.</strong> Adds the G5, 12GB, a 5x telephoto, which used to be reserved for the Pro, and magnetic wireless charging.</p>
<p><strong>Pixel 10 Pro, about Rs 1,76,000.</strong> Larger sensors, 16GB and a brighter LTPO screen in a small body.</p>
<p><strong>Pixel 10 Pro XL, about Rs 2,00,000.</strong> The same with a 6.8-inch screen and bigger battery.</p>

<p>The 10a is the best value if you want a Pixel for photos and updates. The 10 is the best of them overall at this price, thanks to its telephoto. <h2>Tensor: good and not fast</h2>
<p>Google's chip is designed for speech, photo and AI work. Everyday speed is fine. In long games it runs slower and warmer than Snapdragon rivals. If you play Genshin, a OnePlus 15R at less than the price of a Pixel 10 will please you more.</p>
<h2>Network: check before you pay</h2>
<p>Put your own SIM in the phone in the shop and test these:</p>
<ul>
<li>A normal call on 4G. Does it stay on 4G (VoLTE) or fall to an older network?</li>
<li>Mobile data speed.</li>
<li>Whether VoLTE can be turned on in SIM settings for your operator.</li>
<li>eSIM, if it is a United States model without a physical SIM slot. Recent American Pixels may have no tray at all.</li>
</ul>
<p>On unsupported carriers, Google may not enable VoLTE by default. Enthusiasts use tools to turn it on, but you should not have to.</p>
<h2>MDMS</h2>
<p>As there is no official importer, registration depends on the seller. Dial *#06#, check the IMEI in the system, and do not accept a promise. Our <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">guide</a> shows the steps. An unregistered phone can lose service later.</p>
<h2>Warranty and repair</h2>
<p>Google's warranty applies in the country of sale. The shop's warranty is what you get here. Ask for at least six months in writing, with what is covered. Screens and batteries are available through some independent technicians, at a price and after a wait. There have been battery programmes for certain older A-series Pixels that reduce capacity through updates, so research the model before buying used.</p>
<h2>Regional differences</h2>
<p>Japanese and Korean units may have a shutter sound. American ones support certain 5G bands and may be carrier locked. Indian ones are the closest match for our networks. Ask for the model code and look it up on Google's specification page.</p>
<h2>Features that may not work here</h2>
<ul>
<li>Call Screen, Hold for Me and some Phone app features, depending on region.</li>
<li>Google Wallet tap to pay.</li>
<li>Satellite SOS.</li>
<li>Some Gemini features by language and account.</li>

<p>Photography, transcription in supported languages, Circle to Search, Magic Editor and updates work.</p>

<p>Gboard supports Nepali typing, romanised input and voice typing well. Google Translate offers Nepali offline. The assistant understands some Nepali. For language support, a Pixel is as good as any phone and better than most.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging</h2>
<p>Battery life is average to good. Charging is slow by local standards, at 23 to 30W, and 45W on the XL. Use a PD charger with PPS. The Pixel 10 series supports Qi2 magnetic charging.</p>
<h2>Used Pixels</h2>
<p>Used Pixel 7, 8 and 9 phones appear at tempting prices. Check for carrier locks and blacklisted IMEIs from abroad, since phones with unpaid contracts are exported. Open the bootloader menu or developer options: if "OEM unlocking" is greyed out with a carrier note, it may be locked. Look for screen burn, a green line and poor battery. Confirm MDMS. <h2>ZTE and Nubia: who are they?</h2>
<p>ZTE is a large Chinese telecom company whose equipment sits in many operators' networks. Its phones appear under its own name and under Nubia, which began as its premium brand and is now used for nearly everything, from Rs 19,000 phones to gaming devices. REDMAGIC, the brand with fans and lights, is also part of the family. In Nepal the range is cheap to mid-priced, sold through a small network.</p>
<h2>Why consider one?</h2>
<p>Because the hardware for the price is sometimes generous. Two examples: 22.5W charging at Rs 18,999, where others offer 10 to 15W, and an AMOLED 5G phone with 8GB at Rs 33,999. Because it is a lesser-known name, dealers also discount more. And why not? Because there are few service points, few cases and protectors in shops, and few buyers when you resell.</p>
<h2>The A series</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/zte-a37e">A37e</a> at Rs 15,999 has 3GB and is too small for a main phone. The <a href="/products/zte-nubia-a57">Nubia A57</a> at Rs 18,999 is the one worth a look: 120Hz, Android 16 and faster charging. Choose 128GB. The A36 and A56 at nearly the same price have slower screens and charging. The <a href="/products/zte-nubia-a77">A77</a> at Rs 25,499 uses the same basic chip as the A57, and at that price an Infinix Note 50x, Galaxy A07 or Realme C71 is better.</p>
<h2>V80 Max and V80 Design</h2>
<p>Around Rs 30,000. The <a href="/products/zte-nubia-v80-max">V80 Max</a> has a 6.9-inch screen, 6,000mAh and 8GB, but the same T7250 chip. The <a href="/products/zte-nubia-v80-design">V80 Design</a> is slimmer and better-looking. Both cost what a Redmi 15 4G or Infinix Hot 50 Pro Plus does. Those are stronger. The V80s make sense only with a real discount, say Rs 22,000 to 24,000.</p>
<h2>Nubia Air: the stylish one</h2>
<p>At Rs 33,999 the <a href="/products/zte-nubia-air">Nubia Air</a> is the most attractive phone in the range: a slim body, AMOLED at 120Hz, 8GB and a Unisoc T8300 with 5G. It faces the Tecno Spark 40 Pro and Redmi 17 5G. The Tecno's screen is brighter, the Redmi's battery is larger. The Air is the lightest in the hand. If service is available near you, it is a fair choice.</p>
<h2>Neo 3 5G</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/zte-nubia-neo-3-5g">Neo 3 5G</a> at Rs 40,999 is styled for gaming, with shoulder triggers on some versions and bold lines. Its chip is ordinary, and an Infinix Note 50s or GT 30 will run games better. Buy it for triggers if you have tried them and like them.</p>
<h2>The chips explained</h2>
<p>Unisoc supplies most of these phones. The T7200 and T7250 are 12nm parts for basic use. The T7280 is similar. The T8300 is a 6nm 5G chip for everyday speed. None is meant for demanding games. If you have used a phone with a Helio G85, expect about the same from the first group and somewhat more from the last.</p>
<h2>Software: MyOS</h2>
<p>MyOS is fairly clean, close to plain Android, with some tools added. Newer models run Android 15 or 16. Updates are the question: ZTE publishes little about its budget phones, and you should expect security patches for a while and perhaps no new Android version. If you use mobile banking, keep apps updated, avoid unknown APKs and plan to replace the phone in three years.</p>
<h2>Cameras</h2>
<p>Daylight photos are acceptable. Night and motion are weak. The 50MP and 108MP labels on some models refer to single good sensors with decorative companions. Test in the shop's darkest corner.</p>
<h2>Service and spare parts</h2>
<p>Ask the dealer three things. Who is the distributor? Where is the service centre? What does a screen cost, and is it in stock? If the answers are vague, the warranty is vague. In bigger cities, independent technicians can fit batteries and ports, and screens may have to be ordered.</p>
<h2>Accessories</h2>
<p>Cases and tempered glass for Nubia phones are hard to find. Buy them with the phone. Universal flip covers are ugly, and better than a cracked screen.</p>
<h2>MDMS and bills</h2>
<p>Official units are registered. As always, dial *#06#, check the IMEI in the system, and get a VAT bill with the numbers. <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">How to check</a>.</p>

<ul>

<h2>Who should not</h2>
<ul>
<li>Those outside the main cities.</li>

<h2>Keeping it alive</h2>

<h2>Two brands in one</h2>
<p>Blackview makes two very different kinds of phone. The <strong>Wave</strong> series is made of ordinary cheap smartphones. The second has almost none in Nepal, and is the reason to read further.</p>
<h2>What rugged means</h2>
<p>A rugged phone is built to be dropped on concrete, rinsed under a tap and used with wet or gloved hands. It usually has IP68 and IP69K ratings, a frame that stands above the glass, and covers over the ports. Makers also quote MIL-STD-810H, a list of American military test methods for shock, heat, cold and vibration. The maker chooses which tests to perform, so it is a good sign and not a certificate of immortality.</p>
<p>The price is size and weight. These phones weigh 270 to 500 grams and are 13 to 24mm thick. They go in a jacket or on a belt.</p>
<h2>Fort 1: the value choice</h2>
<p>For Rs 21,999 the <a href="/products/blackview-fort-1">Fort 1</a> has a 10,000mAh battery, IP68 and a simple chip. Two or three days between charges is normal. The screen is small and dim by modern standards, the camera is for documentation, and 18W charging means an overnight fill. For a site supervisor, trekking porter, farmer or driver, it does exactly what is needed.</p>
<h2>Fort 100 and BV4800 SE</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/blackview-fort-100">Fort 100</a> at Rs 25,999 is thinner, with 6GB and a normal battery. It is for people who want toughness without the brick. The <a href="/products/blackview-bv4800-se">BV4800 SE</a> at Rs 20,999 is the entry rugged phone. Between the three, the Fort 1's battery makes it the most useful.</p>
<h2>Rock 1 Pro: the power station</h2>
<p>With 15,000mAh, 8GB and 33W charging at Rs 39,999, the <a href="/products/blackview-rock-1-pro">Rock 1 Pro</a> is as much a power bank as a phone. Expect close to a week of light use. It is heavy. For expedition leaders, field scientists and people posted where there is no supply, it can replace a phone and two power banks.</p>
<h2>Wave 2C, 10C, 7C</h2>

The 10C and 7C are large and slow. At Rs 14,000 to 17,000, the Tecno Spark Go 2 and Infinix Smart 10 Plus are better in every way that matters. We would not buy a Wave.</p>
