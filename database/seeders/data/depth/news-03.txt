<p>Samsung Nepal and Ncell have joined hands on a set of offers meant to move people from feature phones to 4G, and to make Galaxy phones easier to pay for. Some of it is marketing. Some of it can save you real money if it matches what you were going to buy anyway. Here is each part, what it is worth, and the questions to ask before you sign.</p>
<h2>The offers in brief</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>2G to 4G exchange.</strong> Bring an old feature phone and receive a bonus of Rs 5,000 on a selected smartphone. Monthly payments begin at about Rs 1,221.</li>
<li><strong>Long EMI.</strong> Plans of 24 to 36 months through Hulas Fin Serve, with 20 per cent down, starting at around Rs 5,106 a month on costlier models.</li>
<li><strong>Extra warranty and exchange bonus.</strong> Up to Rs 10,000 more for your old phone, plus an additional year of warranty, on selected Galaxy Z and S models.</li>
<li><strong>Data packs.</strong> Up to 12 months of Ncell's Rs 799 pack on Z and S phones, and nine months of the Rs 449 pack on A and M phones.</li>
<li><strong>App-based valuation.</strong> A tool called PhoneShield used at service centres and selected stores to check a phone before exchange.</li>
</ul>
<p>Offers of this sort change by month and by store. Treat the figures as the shape of the programme and confirm the detail at the counter.</p>
<h2>Is the free data worth what they say?</h2>
<p>Twelve packs of Rs 799 come to Rs 9,588 on paper. Their worth to you depends on your habits. If you already buy that pack every month, it is real cash in your pocket. If you use Wi-Fi all day and buy a Rs 200 pack twice a month, the value is closer to Rs 400 a month. If your main SIM is from NTC, it is worth whatever you would pay for a second line, which may be nothing.</p>
<p>There are conditions to look for. The packs are usually credited month by month, must be activated by a date, and may be tied to the number registered at purchase. Ask what happens if you miss a month.</p>
<h2>Rs 5,000 for a feature phone</h2>
<p>This is the most generous part for the people it targets. A keypad phone that works sells for a few hundred rupees. Getting Rs 5,000 for it against a 4G smartphone is a real subsidy, provided the smartphone's price has not been raised to pay for it. Check the normal price on our product pages first. A <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a07">Galaxy A07</a> has been about Rs 25,499. If the exchange brings it close to Rs 20,000, take it.</p>
<p>Before handing in the old phone, copy its contacts to the SIM or write them down, remove the memory card and delete messages. It may go to recycling, and recycling does not mean that the data is destroyed.</p>
<h2>Thirty-six months is a long time</h2>
<p>Long plans make a flagship look affordable. Take the <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s26">Galaxy S26</a> at Rs 1,39,999. With 20 per cent down you pay Rs 28,000 on the day and finance Rs 1,12,000. Over 36 months at an ordinary finance rate, the total interest can be Rs 20,000 or more, though the monthly amount looks gentle. Three questions decide whether the plan is good:</p>
<ol>
<li>What is the total of all payments, including the fee?</li>
<li>Is the cash price the same, or is there a discount that EMI buyers lose?</li>
<li>What happens if a payment is late or the phone is stolen?</li>
</ol>
<p>The last one matters in the third year. You may still be paying for a phone that is broken. For that reason we prefer plans of twelve months, or eighteen at most, and a phone whose price lets you manage that. The EMI estimate on our product pages shows the monthly figure for different periods.</p>
<h2>An extra year of warranty</h2>
<p>For a Galaxy Z this is the best item on the list. Foldables have more to go wrong, and a hinge or an inner screen outside warranty costs a large part of the phone. Read what the second year covers. Manufacturing faults are normal, accidents usually not. Ask whether the extension is registered in Samsung's system by IMEI and can be seen in the Members app. A stamp on paper is weaker.</p>
<h2>How valuation apps work</h2>
<p>PhoneShield and similar tools test the screen, touch, cameras, buttons, speakers, battery and connectivity, look at the model and storage, and offer a price. They bring some fairness, since the figure no longer depends on the mood of the person at the counter. They also find faults you have stopped noticing: a dead microphone, a swollen battery, a line on the screen.</p>
<p>Before you go, clean the phone, charge it, remove the case and the cracked protector, and sign out of your accounts. Look up private sale prices for your model. A clean phone often fetches 15 to 25 per cent more in a private sale, at the cost of time and some risk. Exchange is worth it when the bonus closes that gap.</p>
<h2>AI features on cheaper phones</h2>
<p>Part of the campaign promotes Galaxy AI on models such as the <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a17-5g">Galaxy A17 5G</a>. Be clear about what that means. Budget phones get a short list, for example Circle to Search and some assistant features, most of which need the internet. Call translation, generative photo editing and note summaries belong to the S and Z series and some higher A models. If a seller mentions AI, ask to see the feature working on the phone in question.</p>
<h2>Who benefits most</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Families moving a parent from a keypad phone.</strong> The exchange bonus is excellent.</li>
<li><strong>Ncell users who already buy monthly packs.</strong> The data is close to its face value.</li>
<li><strong>Foldable buyers.</strong> The warranty is worth having.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who benefits least</h2>
<ul>
<li>People who mainly use NTC.</li>
<li>Cash buyers who can bargain for a discount in place of gifts.</li>
<li>Anyone who would stretch to a costlier phone because the instalment looks small.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What about the 4G argument?</h2>
<p>Operators want customers off 2G for good reasons. Old networks occupy valuable frequencies and serve few people well. VoLTE calls on 4G are clearer and connect faster. For users, the change brings wallets, video calls with children abroad and government services. It also brings scams. If you are setting up a first smartphone for an older relative, take an hour to turn on automatic updates and Play Protect, block unknown app installs, put a lock on the wallet app, and explain that nobody from a bank ever asks for an OTP.</p>
<h2>Compare before you accept</h2>
<p>A package of gifts can hide a higher price. Take the final amount you would pay, subtract only the things you would have bought anyway, and compare the result with the same phone at two other shops and with rivals. At Rs 45,000, for example, a <a href="/products/redmi-note-15-5g">Redmi Note 15 5G</a> or <a href="/products/moto-g96">Moto G96</a> offers stronger hardware without any gifts. <h2>Questions to take to the counter</h2>
<ol>
<li>Which of these offers apply to this exact model in this shop today?</li>
<li>Can exchange, bank discount and data offers be used together?</li>
<li>How and when is the data credited, and to which number?</li>
<li>Is the phone an official Nepali unit, registered in MDMS?</li>

<p>It is a useful programme for two groups: people leaving feature phones and people who were about to buy a premium Galaxy on an Ncell number. For others it is a pleasant extra at best. <p>The closest relative is Huawei's own Pura X View, and the two share a lot. OSTA appears to be Huawei's way of selling the idea under a fresh name to a younger audience.</p>
<h2>Why a wide phone?</h2>
<p>Tall screens are good for scrolling. They are poor for nearly everything else. A 16:9 video on a tall phone leaves black bars at both ends. A PDF page must be zoomed. Two apps side by side are ribbons. A spreadsheet shows three columns.</p>
<p>A wide screen gives a page, a map or a video more room without folding. At 87mm across, the OSTA shows an A5 page at a comfortable size. Reading news, marking a document, following a map while riding in a taxi and watching a film all improve. In some ways it offers half of what a foldable offers, without a hinge and at less weight.</p>
<h2>And why not?</h2>
<p>Hands. The average adult can wrap fingers around about 75mm. Above 80mm, one-handed typing becomes a stretch, and 87mm is wider than any ordinary phone sold in Nepal. You will use it with two hands, like a small tablet. Trouser pockets designed for narrow phones may object. Cases and protectors will be scarce. And apps made for tall screens may look stretched until developers adapt them.</p>
<p>People with large hands, readers and those who keep a phone in a bag will not mind. Commuters holding a handle on a bus will.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging in a thin body</h2>
<p>Seven thousand milliampere-hours in a 6.68mm body shows how far silicon-carbon cells have come. A conventional battery of that capacity would need a phone two millimetres thicker. Wired charging at 66W and wireless at 50W are both quick, though you need Huawei's own chargers to see those figures. For a screen of this size, a day and a half of ordinary use is a fair expectation.</p>
<h2>Cameras</h2>
<p>Huawei has made some of the best phone cameras of the past decade. A 200MP main sensor with a 50MP periscope suggests that the OSTA is not a cheap experiment. We have no measurements and no pictures of our own to judge, so anything more would be guesswork.</p>
<h2>The software question</h2>
<p>Huawei phones sold in China run HarmonyOS, and those sold elsewhere run EMUI. Neither includes Google's services. In practice that means no Play Store, no built-in Google Maps, Gmail or YouTube apps, and banking or wallet apps that may refuse to start. There are workarounds, including Huawei's AppGallery, the Aurora Store and tools that imitate Google's services. Some people live with them happily. For a Nepali user who depends on eSewa, Khalti, a bank's app and Google Maps, it is a risk to check in advance, app by app.</p>
<p>The newest Chinese version, HarmonyOS without Android inside, cannot run Android apps at all except through a compatibility layer. A phone imported from China with that system would be a poor choice here, whatever its hardware.</p>
<h2>Will it come to Nepal?</h2>
<p>Huawei has not announced it. The company sells watches, bands, earbuds and some other devices here, and its phones have been rare for years. If the OSTA line goes global, it would probably appear first in the Middle East and South-East Asia. Importers may bring a few units. <p>If the 7,000mAh battery is what caught your eye, the Nepali market is full of them. <p>Wide phones have been tried before. BlackBerry's square Passport of 2014 was loved by a few and bought by fewer. LG made phones with a 4:3 screen. Two things are different today. Foldables have pushed developers to support many shapes, and video is what most people do on a phone. Perhaps the time has come. Perhaps it will join the museum.</p>

<h2>Understanding battery numbers</h2>
<p>A milliampere-hour figure tells you the size of the tank. How long it lasts depends on the engine and the driver. A bright 6.99-inch screen uses more than a 6.5-inch one. An old chip on weak signal uses more than a new one on good signal. As a rough guide for ordinary use:</p>
<ul>
<li>5,000mAh: a day.</li>
<li>6,000 to 6,500mAh: a day and a half.</li>
<li>7,000 to 8,000mAh: close to two days.</li>
<li>10,000mAh: two to three days.</li>
</ul>
<p>Heavy users should halve those. Beyond 8,000mAh, charging speed begins to matter more than capacity, because filling such a battery at 18W takes half the night. At 45W, 8,100mAh would need around an hour and a half.</p>
<h2>Weight</h2>
<p>Large batteries are heavy, and the Note 17 5G is already a substantial phone. Hold one for ten minutes as if you were reading in bed. If your wrist complains, a 190 gram phone with 6,000mAh will make you happier than a 230 gram one with 8,000.</p>
<h2>Silicon-carbon and ageing</h2>
<p>The new cells pack more energy by adding silicon to the negative electrode. Silicon swells slightly as it charges, and makers manage that with the structure of the material. Most promise that the battery keeps around 80 per cent of its capacity after a thousand or more cycles. With a two-day battery, a thousand cycles is over five years. Keep it cool, avoid leaving it empty, and use the charge limit at night.</p>
<h2>What a true upgrade would look like</h2>
<p>We would like to see the next Redmi Note with a faster chip of the Snapdragon 6 class, UFS 3.1 storage, 8GB as the minimum and the Note 15's camera. Put those into the Note 17's body and nobody would care about 400mAh. If the leak is right, that phone is not coming this time, at least not in the basic model. The Pro versions may be different.</p>
<h2>The POCO connection</h2>
<p>Xiaomi sells many phones twice, with a different badge and a different price. If a POCO version appears, compare it carefully. Sometimes it costs Rs 2,000 to 3,000 less with the same hardware and a brighter back. Service is the same in practice, as POCO phones are handled by Xiaomi's centres.</p>
<h2>Official units</h2>
<p>Redmi Notes are among the most imported phones across the open border. An Indian unit may be a few thousand rupees cheaper, without Nepali warranty and with extra steps for MDMS. Check the IMEI and the sticker of the distributor. <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">This guide</a> shows how.</p>
<h2>If you own an older Redmi Note</h2>
<p>From a Note 10, 11 or 12, any phone mentioned here will feel new. From a Note 13 Pro or 14 Pro, the basic Note 17 or 18 may be a step down in speed and camera. Replace the battery for Rs 2,500 to 4,000 and wait for a Pro model on sale.</p>
<h2>Our verdict on the rumour</h2>
<p>It is believable and unimportant. The Note 18 5G, if this is it, will be a fine phone for the same people who like the Note 17 5G, at a similar or slightly higher price. Buy the current one at a discount, or better, look at the Note 15 5G for speed or the Pro Max for battery. We will add the new model to the catalogue when it has an official specification, and not before.</p>

=== honor-13500mah-battery-phone-certified replace
<p>A Honor battery with a typical capacity of 13,500mAh has passed safety certification in China. Certification papers do not name a phone, but they do mean that a cell of that size is close to production. Five years ago this was the capacity of a power bank. Here is what is known, how such a battery fits in a pocket, and whether it would change anything for someone living in Nepal.</p>
<h2>What is known</h2>
<ul>
<li>The battery is rated at 13,300mAh, with a typical 13,500mAh.</li>
<li>It appears under model number HB6674118ELW-C in China's 3C system.</li>
<li>It is expected in a 7-inch mid-range phone, possibly from the Power series.</li>
<li>Honor's earlier large models used 10,080mAh, 11,000mAh and 12,070mAh cells, and the company is said to be trying 14,000 to 15,000mAh.</li>
</ul>
<p>There is no name, date, price or mention of any market outside China. Everything beyond the certificate is inference.</p>
<h2>How can a phone hold that much?</h2>
<p>Ordinary lithium-ion batteries use graphite for the negative electrode. Silicon can hold roughly ten times more lithium by weight, but it swells and cracks. The solution in use today mixes silicon with carbon in a structure that gives it room to expand. Each year the share of silicon has gone up, and the energy in a given volume with it. A phone from 2021 with 5,000mAh and one from 2026 with 8,000mAh can be the same size.</p>
<p>At 13,500mAh the phone will still be large. Expect a 7-inch screen and a weight of 230 grams or more. Honor has been clever about thickness, so a body under 9mm is possible.</p>
<h2>How long would it last?</h2>
<p>Nobody outside Honor has measured it. We can reason from phones on sale. The <a href="/products/realme-p4-power">Realme P4 Power</a>, with 10,001mAh, lasts about two days for heavy users and three for light ones. A third more capacity, with a similar screen and chip, points to three days for many people and four for those who mostly call and chat. Continuous video might approach thirty hours.</p>
<h2>Who needs three days?</h2>
<p><strong>Trekking guides and porters.</strong> Charging in tea houses costs Rs 300 to 1,000 a time at altitude, and cold drains batteries faster. A phone that crosses from Lukla to Gorak Shep on one or two charges pays for itself.</p>
<p><strong>Drivers.</strong> Long-distance bus and truck drivers with maps and calls all day.</p>
<p><strong>Field staff.</strong> Surveyors, health workers, journalists, vaccinators.</p>
<p><strong>People in places with poor supply.</strong> Fewer now than ten years ago, though there are still many.</p>
<p><strong>Migrant workers.</strong> Long shifts on sites where charging is not allowed.</p>
<p>For someone with an office and a bedside table, 6,000mAh is enough. Charging is a habit, not a hardship.</p>
<h2>The other side of a giant battery</h2>
<p><strong>Weight.</strong> You will feel it in a shirt pocket and while reading in bed.</p>
<p><strong>Charging time.</strong> At 80W, around an hour and a quarter. At 18W with an old charger, five or six hours.</p>
<p><strong>Air travel.</strong> The limit for batteries in devices is 100 watt-hours without approval. At about 3.85 volts, 13,500mAh is some 52 watt-hours, so a phone like this is fine in the cabin. Spare power banks of 20,000mAh at 74 watt-hours are within the limit as well, and must be in hand luggage.</p>
<p><strong>Repair.</strong> A large special cell costs more and may have to be ordered. Never fit an unknown replacement in a phone that stores this much energy.</p>
<p><strong>Price.</strong> More battery in a mid-range phone usually means a plainer camera.</p>
<h2>Phone or power bank?</h2>
<p>A fair question. A 10,000mAh power bank costs Rs 1,500 to 3,000, and delivers about 6,000 to 6,500mAh to your phone after conversion losses. It weighs 200 grams, and you leave it at home on ordinary days. A phone with a huge battery is one object, charges faster and never needs a cable in the rain. If you need endurance once a month, buy the power bank. If you need it every day, buy the phone. See <a href="/products?category=accessory">accessories</a> for current prices.</p>
<h2>Reverse charging</h2>
<p>Big-battery phones increasingly charge other devices by cable at 20W or so. With 13,500mAh, you could fill a friend's phone and still have two days left. For a family on a trip, one such phone serves as everyone's emergency supply.</p>

<h2>Would Honor bring it to Nepal?</h2>
<p>Honor has been active here, with phones from Rs 16,000 to more than a lakh, and its strength has been battery and toughness. A phone with this battery would suit the market well. It may appear under another name and with a smaller cell, as happens with many Chinese models. The X9 series usually arrives within months of its global launch. A domestic Power model may never come. Importers will certainly try, and an imported unit means no local warranty and a check of MDMS.</p>
<h2>Is it safe?</h2>
<p>Certification exists to answer that. Cells are tested for short circuit, crushing, heat and overcharging. More energy does mean more consequences when something is abused. So the ordinary rules matter more: use a good charger, do not cover the phone while it charges at high speed, do not use it after the body swells or bends, and do not let an unknown shop fit a battery. A phone that becomes too hot to hold should be unplugged and left on a hard surface.</p>
<h2>How it ages</h2>
<p>Batteries wear by cycles and by heat. A phone charged twice a week instead of every day goes through a third as many cycles. After four years it may still hold most of its capacity, while a small-battery phone is on its second cell. Endurance, then, is also longevity, a quiet argument in favour of the giants.</p>

If you need endurance now, a 10,000mAh phone at Rs 70,000 is already more than most people can finish in two days. If you can wait a year, 8,000mAh will be ordinary at Rs 40,000.
