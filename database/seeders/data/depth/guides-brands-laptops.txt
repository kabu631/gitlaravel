<h2>How to read an Asus model number</h2>
<p>X1504VA looks like noise, and it is a small description. The first letter is the family: X for Vivobook, UX or UM for Zenbook with Intel or AMD, FX and FA for TUF with Intel or AMD, G for ROG. The next two digits are the screen size, 15 or 14. The following digits are the generation, and the letters at the end describe the platform. So X1504VA is a 15-inch Vivobook with a 13th generation Intel chip. Write down the whole code before comparing prices. "Vivobook 15" alone describes a dozen machines.</p>
<h2>Vivobook: choose by memory</h2>
<p>Asus sells Vivobooks with 8GB at Rs 90,000 and with 16GB at Rs 80,000, depending on age and importer. The 16GB X1504VA is the bargain of the range, as we explain in <a href="/guides/best-laptops-under-80000-nepal">laptops under Rs 80,000</a>. The Vivobook S versions are slimmer with bigger batteries and better screens. The <a href="/products/asus-vivobook-s14-m3407ha-2025">S14 with a Ryzen 5 220</a> and 70Wh is a fine student machine, at a price that should be negotiated.</p>
<h2>Zenbook: the thin ones</h2>
<p>Zenbooks have metal bodies, OLED screens and good keyboards. The cheap one, the <a href="/products/asus-zenbook-14-oled-q415">Q415</a>, has 8GB and an imported past, described in <a href="/reviews/asus-zenbook-14-oled-q415-review-nepal">our review</a>. The current ones cost Rs 2 lakh and more and compete with the MacBook Air. Look for 16GB or more and at least 70Wh.</p>
<h2>TUF: sturdy gaming</h2>
<p>TUF laptops take knocks, cool well and cost less than ROG. The A15 with an RTX 3050 is getting old, as <a href="/reviews/asus-tuf-gaming-a15-rtx-3050-review-nepal">our review</a> says. The A16 and F16 with RTX 4050 to 5060 are the ones to consider. In this family, ask for the graphics wattage and the panel's colour coverage, since both vary.</p>
<h2>ROG: from fast to absurd</h2>
<p>Strix models are thick and powerful. The <a href="/products/asus-rog-strix-g18-g814fp-2025-ryzen-9-9955hx-rtx-5070">Strix G18 with a Ryzen 9 and RTX 5070</a> at about Rs 4 lakh with 32GB is better value than several others with the same graphics chip at Rs 4.6 lakh or more. Zephyrus models are thin and lovely, and our <a href="/news/asus-rog-zephyrus-g16-2025-rtx-5080-benchmarks-leaked">Zephyrus G16 article</a> compares them. The Zephyrus Duo has two screens and costs as much as a small car.</p>
<h2>V16 and Gaming V16</h2>
<p>A newer family that puts gaming chips in plainer bodies. The <a href="/products/asus-v16-2025-core-7-rtx-5050">V16 with an RTX 5050</a> at about Rs 2,08,000 is close to a Lenovo LOQ in idea. Check the watts and the screen.</p>
<h2>ProArt and others</h2>
<p>ProArt laptops are for creators, with accurate OLED screens and dials. Few reach Nepal. If one does, look at who provides the warranty. Chromebooks and ExpertBooks appear now and then.</p>
<h2>Which Asus for which person?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>School or first laptop:</strong> Vivobook Go with a Core i3 or Ryzen 3, 8GB and an SSD of 256GB or more. Avoid the Celeron.</li>
<li><strong>Bachelor student:</strong> Vivobook 15 X1504VA with 16GB.</li>
<li><strong>Office and travel:</strong> Vivobook S14 or Zenbook 14.</li>
<li><strong>Engineering and games:</strong> TUF A16 or V16 with an RTX 5050.</li>
<li><strong>Creators:</strong> Zephyrus or ProArt with 32GB.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Traps in the Asus range</h2>
<ol>
<li>The Vivobook Go E410 with a Celeron and 64GB of eMMC. It is slow the day you buy it.</li>
<li>The <a href="/reviews/asus-vivobook-14-x1404va-i3-review-nepal">X1404VA with 128GB</a>, which needs a new drive at once.</li>
<li>The <a href="/reviews/asus-vivobook-14-x1404vap-review-nepal">X1404VAP</a>, sold with two different drives under one name.</li>
<li>16GB in laptops above Rs 4 lakh with soldered memory.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Screens</h2>
<p>Asus uses everything from dull 45% NTSC panels to reference-class OLED. On Vivobooks, look at the panel with your own eyes. On OLED models, set pixel care and dark mode, as static toolbars over years can leave a mark.</p>
<h2>MyAsus: use it</h2>
<p>The app registers the warranty, limits charging to 60 or 80 per cent, updates drivers and runs diagnostics. On gaming models, Armoury Crate controls fans and power. Remove the trial antivirus, as Windows Security is enough.</p>
<h2>Warranty in Nepal</h2>
<p>Asus has an authorised distributor and service in Nepal. Official units usually carry one or two years, and some include accidental cover if registered within a period. Units with codes for other regions, such as Q-series Zenbooks or North American ROG models, are imports with a shop warranty. Enter the serial number on the Asus site to see. Ask for the answer in writing.</p>
<h2>Power and heat</h2>
<p>Use the original charger. Some Vivobooks take 45W by a round plug, others USB-C. TUF and ROG machines need 150 to 280W, more than many small inverters supply cleanly. Clean the fans each year, and raise the rear of gaming models in summer.</p>
<h2>Upgrades</h2>
<p>Many Vivobooks have some memory soldered and one slot. TUF and Strix models have two slots and two M.2 bays, making them the most upgradeable. Zenbooks and Zephyrus are fixed except for the SSD. Check before buying, since it changes how much memory you need on the first day.</p>
<h2>Asus or another brand?</h2>
<p><strong>Lenovo</strong> has better keyboards and the best-value gaming laptop in the LOQ. <strong>HP</strong> and <strong>Dell</strong> have stronger office service. <strong>Acer</strong> is cheaper for similar parts. <strong>MSI</strong> is for gaming with bolder designs. Asus offers the widest choice, OLED at lower prices, and sturdy gaming machines.</p>
<h2>Price behaviour</h2>
<p>With so many models there is always something being cleared. Older Vivobooks and TUFs with good specifications appear at Rs 5,000 to 20,000 below newer, weaker ones. Ask what is old in stock. Discounts are larger after Tihar and before the new college sessions.</p>
<h2>Checks in the shop</h2>
<ul>
<li>Full model code on the base matches the box and bill.</li>
<li>Processor, memory and drive in Task Manager.</li>
<li>Battery report with powercfg.</li>
<li>Screen on plain white, grey and black.</li>
<li>Hinge, keyboard, webcam and all ports.</li>
</ul>
<h2>In short</h2>
<p>Asus has a right laptop for nearly everyone and a wrong one beside it on the shelf. Let memory, screen and model code guide you, and not the family name. For advice that crosses brands, see our <a href="/guides/best-laptops-nepal-students-gamers">laptop guide</a>.</p>
