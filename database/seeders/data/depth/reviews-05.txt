=== moto-g96-review-nepal
<h2>Specifications</h2>
[[specs:moto-g96]]
<h2>A phone for people who hold phones</h2>
<p>Spec sheets reward big numbers. Hands reward other things. At 178 grams with a curved screen, the G96 is one of the few phones under Rs 50,000 that you enjoy picking up. The edges slope into the palm, the back is soft vegan leather on most versions, and it slides into a jeans pocket without dragging it down. If you have been carrying a 220 gram phone with an enormous battery, the difference on the first day is almost comic.</p>
<h2>Screen</h2>
<p>The 6.67-inch pOLED at 144Hz is the headline. Motion is fluid, blacks are deep and colours are rich. The curved sides look expensive, with the usual costs: reflections along the edges, protectors that cost more and fit less well, and an occasional accidental touch. Use a case that covers the sides and those touches mostly go away.</p>
<p>Peak brightness of 1,600 nits belongs to HDR video. In ordinary sunlight it is good, though phones such as the Honor X8d are brighter.</p>
<h2>Battery: check the number</h2>
<p>Our text above says 5,000mAh, while the specification sheet in our catalogue lists 5,500mAh. Motorola has sold the G96 with the larger figure, so expect that, and look at the label on the box. Either way it is a one-day phone. A commuter with moderate use will end the day with a quarter left. A student who streams on mobile data between classes will need a top-up. Charging at 30W takes a little over an hour.</p>
<h2>Performance</h2>
<p>The Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 is two generations old by name and still a strong middle-class chip. Apps open quickly, multitasking on 8GB is smooth and light games run at 60 frames per second or more. PUBG Mobile is enjoyable at balanced settings. The UFS 2.2 storage is slower than the UFS 3.1 and 4.0 found in gaming phones, which you notice only when installing something large.</p>
<h2>Cameras</h2>
<p>A 50MP Sony LYT-700C main sensor with optical stabilisation is a better part than usual at this price. Photos have good detail and pleasing colour, and night shots are usable without a tripod. The 8MP ultra wide doubles as a macro camera with autofocus. The 32MP front camera is sharp, and it helps with video interviews and reels. Video goes to 4K at 30 frames per second on both sides.</p>
<h2>Software: clean, with a short horizon</h2>
<p>Motorola's Android feels calm. There are few extra apps, notifications behave, and gestures such as chopping the phone twice for the torch are addictive. Smart Connect lets you use the phone with a TV or PC like a small desktop. The weakness is support. Motorola promises one or two Android upgrades and three to four years of patches on most G phones, less than Samsung gives its cheaper A series. The G96 launched with Android 15. Ask the seller what the plan is for this model.</p>
<h2>Toughness</h2>
<p>The G96 is rated IP68 in Motorola's own material for several markets, which is a pleasant surprise in a light phone. Confirm it for the unit you are buying, as regional versions differ, and remember that no warranty covers water.</p>
<h2>Compared with its neighbours</h2>
[[rivals:moto-g96]]
<p>The <a href="/products/redmi-note-15-5g">Redmi Note 15 5G</a> is the closest all-round rival, with a brighter screen and wider service. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-m17-5g">Galaxy M17 5G</a> and <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a17-5g">A17 5G</a> win on support and lose on speed and smoothness. The <a href="/products/tecno-pova-6">Tecno Pova 6</a> has a bigger battery with bold styling. If endurance is the main goal, the <a href="/products/honor-x8d">Honor X8d</a> is cheaper and lasts far longer.</p>
<h2>Service in Nepal</h2>
<p>Motorola has fewer service points than Samsung, Xiaomi or vivo. Curved pOLED panels are not cheap, and a corner shop is unlikely to stock one. Before buying, ask the seller for the cost and waiting time of a screen replacement and where it would be done. If you live far from Kathmandu, weigh this seriously.</p>
<h2>Who will love it</h2>
<ul>
<li>People who want a light, good-looking phone.</li>
<li>Users who dislike bloated software.</li>
<li>Students who take photos and video more than they play games.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who will not</h2>
<ul>
<li>Anyone away from a charger all day.</li>
<li>Owners who keep a phone for five years.</li>
<li>People who break screens.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Small print before you pay</h2>
<p>Only the 8GB/256GB version is listed in Nepal, which is the right one to have. Check that a charger is included, that the IMEI appears in MDMS, and that the phone is not an Indian import with a different warranty. Try the curved edges with your own grip for a few minutes. And look at last year's <a href="/products/motorola-edge-50-fusion">Edge 50 Fusion</a> at a slightly higher price, which brings better protection and a similar screen.</p>
<h2>Verdict in a sentence or two</h2>
<p>The Moto G96 proves that a phone under Rs 45,000 can feel expensive. It asks you to charge nightly and to accept shorter support, and in return it is nicer to use every hour in between.</p>

=== samsung-galaxy-m17-5g-review-nepal
<h2>Specifications</h2>
[[specs:samsung-galaxy-m17-5g]]
<h2>M or A? Sorting out Samsung's twins</h2>
<p>Samsung sells two nearly identical phones at this price. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a17-5g">Galaxy A17 5G</a> is the one in shop windows, and the M17 5G is usually sold online. Both use the Exynos 1330, a 6.7-inch 90Hz Super AMOLED, a 5,000mAh battery with 25W charging and the same trio of cameras. The M series is typically a little cheaper or offers a little more memory for the money. When the prices are within a thousand rupees, buy whichever has the better warranty and is easier to return.</p>
<p>Listings for the M17 in Nepal also differ. Our price table records 6GB/128GB at Rs 43,999, whereas the specification sheet mentions 8GB. Confirm what is in the box before you pay.</p>
<h2>What the AMOLED does for you</h2>
<p>Many phones around Rs 40,000 still use LCDs. Super AMOLED brings deeper contrast, better viewing angles and an always-on clock. Full HD+ keeps small Devanagari text readable. At 1,100 nits in high-brightness mode, it copes with daylight. The 90Hz refresh rate is the weak point next to 120Hz and 144Hz rivals, though it is smooth enough when it is your only phone.</p>
<h2>Day to day</h2>
<p>The Exynos 1330 is built on a 5nm process and belongs to the entry level of 5G chips. WhatsApp, Facebook, YouTube, bank apps and video calls are fine. Opening many apps in a hurry will show small delays. Games run at low to medium settings. If you compare it with the Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 in the <a href="/products/moto-g96">Moto G96</a>, the Motorola is clearly faster in everything that takes effort.</p>
<h2>Cameras: the stabilised one counts</h2>
<p>The 50MP main camera has optical stabilisation, which is uncommon here. It produces steady photos and calmer video. The 5MP ultra wide is limited to daylight, and the macro lens can be ignored. The 13MP selfie camera is ordinary. Samsung's processing brightens faces, and most people like the result straight from the camera.</p>
<h2>Battery</h2>
<p>A full day is normal, with light users reaching into the second. At 25W a full charge takes about ninety minutes, and the charger is usually sold separately. A phone with 7,000mAh lasts noticeably longer, so if your power supply is uncertain, look at the <a href="/products/honor-x8d">Honor X8d</a>.</p>
<h2>Software support is why this phone exists</h2>
<p>Samsung's promise for its recent cheaper phones covers several Android versions and years of security patches, far beyond the typical budget phone. There are two benefits. Your wallet and bank apps will keep working on a secure version, and the phone will be easier to sell. Samsung also adds Knox security, Secure Folder for private files and Auto Blocker against dangerous installs. For a family phone these are useful tools, and not just words on a box.</p>
<h2>Service everywhere</h2>
<p>Samsung's service network is the widest in the country. You can get a screen in Pokhara, Biratnagar or Nepalgunj without sending the phone away. Genuine AMOLED parts cost more than LCD ones, so budget for a case and protector.</p>
<h2>How it compares</h2>
[[rivals:samsung-galaxy-m17-5g]]
<p>The <a href="/products/redmi-note-15-5g">Redmi Note 15 5G</a> at the same price has a smoother, brighter screen and faster charging. The <a href="/products/realme-p3">Realme P3</a> is much faster. The <a href="/products/vivo-v60-lite-4g">vivo V60 Lite 4G</a> is slim, with quick charging and no 5G. If you would rather have hardware than support, choose among those. If the phone is going to a parent, a child or an employee, the Samsung is easier to defend.</p>
<h2>A few honest scenarios</h2>
<p><strong>You sell on Facebook and need a reliable work phone.</strong> The M17 is good. The main camera handles product photos, the screen shows colours accurately enough and repairs are easy.</p>
<p><strong>You are a gamer with Rs 45,000.</strong> Skip it. The Realme P3 or a POCO on offer will serve you better.</p>
<p><strong>You are buying for your mother.</strong> A fine choice, though the simpler <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a07">Galaxy A07</a> costs far less if she only calls and chats.</p>
<p><strong>You want it for five years.</strong> Choose the version with the most memory, as the chip will not get any faster.</p>
<h2>Setup advice</h2>
<ul>
<li>Turn on Auto Blocker and automatic updates.</li>
<li>Use Secure Folder for bank and wallet apps if you share the phone with children.</li>
<li>Set battery protection to 80 per cent if you charge every night.</li>
<li>Uninstall or disable partner apps you do not use.</li>
<li>Choose 90Hz, which Samsung sometimes leaves off.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Waiting for a sale</h2>
<p>Online-focused phones are discounted often. Watch for Dashain, New Year and brand-day sales, and compare the final price after delivery and bank offers. At around Rs 38,000 to 40,000 the M17 becomes attractive. At Rs 44,000, the gap to a much better phone is small.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>It is a decent Samsung at a fair price, not a great phone at a great price. You are paying for AMOLED, stabilised photos, service in your own city and years of updates. If those four words describe what you need, buy it with confidence. If you stopped reading at the refresh rate, you already know your answer.</p>

<p><strong>Support.</strong> Six Android upgrades and six years of patches lead into 2032. Almost nothing else at this price comes close.</p>
<p><strong>Screen.</strong> The 6.7-inch Super AMOLED at 120Hz is bright, accurate and comfortable.</p>
<p><strong>Main camera.</strong> A stabilised 50MP sensor with Samsung's mature processing gives dependable photos of family and food in most light.</p>

<h2>Which version makes sense?</h2>

The <a href="/products/oneplus-nord-5">OnePlus Nord 5</a> is quick with clean software, and the <a href="/products/oneplus-nord-ce-6">Nord CE 6</a> lasts for days. With autofocus you can hold the phone near to talk or far away for a group, and faces stay crisp. OPPO's skin processing is smooth by default, and it can be reduced in the settings.</p>
<h2>Rear cameras</h2>
<p>The 50MP main camera with stabilisation handles daylight and ordinary indoor scenes well. The 8MP ultra wide is average. The 2MP macro has no purpose. There is no telephoto. Portrait mode blurs backgrounds convincingly around hair and glasses, one of OPPO's strengths.</p>
<h2>The processor question</h2>
<p>The Snapdragon 6 Gen 1 is a 4nm chip that first appeared in 2022. It still runs ordinary apps smoothly and keeps the phone cool. The issue is what it will feel like in 2029, and what others give you for Rs 63,999. The <a href="/products/poco-x7-pro">POCO X7 Pro</a> is several times faster in games and cheaper. If you do not play games or edit video, you may never notice. If you do, you will notice every day.</p>
<h2>ColorOS 16</h2>
<p>OPPO's software has become one of the smoothest on Android. Animations are fluid, the control centre is neat and the privacy tools are useful, for example a private safe and the option to give apps empty data. There are some bundled apps and a recommendation feed to switch off. Connection with iPhones for sharing photos through a companion app is handy in mixed families. OPPO's promised support for Reno F models is moderate. Ask for the number of Android versions.</p>
<h2>Battery life</h2>
<p>Either version will last a full day, and the 7,000mAh one closer to two. With 80W charging, a short stop fills most of it. Use the supplied charger. OPPO's fast charging is proprietary, and other chargers will be slower.</p>
<h2>What else costs about the same</h2>
[[rivals:oppo-reno-15-f]]
<p>The <a href="/products/redmi-note-15-pro-plus">Redmi Note 15 Pro Plus</a> offers a strong main camera and tougher protection. The <a href="/products/xiaomi-15t">Xiaomi 15T</a> brings Leica-tuned triple cameras and a much better chip. The <a href="/products/nothing-phone-4b">Nothing Phone 4b</a> has clean software and unusual design. The <a href="/products/vivo-v70-fe">vivo V70 FE</a> is the closest in spirit, aimed at portraits and looks. The <a href="/products/redmi-note-17-pro-max-5g">Redmi Note 17 Pro Max</a> is about battery. The Reno's advantages over all of them are size, style and front camera focus.</p>
<h2>Two honest profiles</h2>
<p><strong>Sita, 24, works in a bank and posts reels on weekends.</strong> She wants a slim phone with a good front camera, does not play games and changes phones every two years. The Reno 15 F is a reasonable choice if she gets the better version with a discount.</p>
<p><strong>Bikash, 20, studies engineering and plays PUBG.</strong> He should not look at it twice.</p>
<h2>Price and offers</h2>
<p>OPPO dealers are flexible. Accessories, screen cover, exchange bonuses and cash discounts are common, and the price on the board is an opening bid. At around Rs 55,000 the 8GB/256GB model becomes fair. The 12GB/512GB version at Rs 69,999 is not, as it enters the range of far faster phones.</p>
<h2>Service</h2>
<p>OPPO has an established service network in Nepal with centres in major cities and reasonable parts prices. It is one of the better brands in that respect after Samsung, Xiaomi and vivo. Keep the bill and the warranty card.</p>
<h2>Before you buy</h2>
<ul>
<li>Check the box for battery, charging and front camera details.</li>
<li>Try the front camera indoors, where weak cameras show themselves.</li>
<li>Hold it next to a larger phone. If compactness does not matter to you, the argument for it weakens.</li>
<li>Confirm memory, storage and MDMS registration.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>The Reno 15 F is a pleasant phone with a clear personality, carrying a processor that does not match its price. Choose it for size, style and selfies, at a discount, in the better-equipped version. If any of those conditions fails, there are stronger phones for less money.</p>
