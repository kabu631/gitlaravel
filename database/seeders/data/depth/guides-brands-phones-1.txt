=== samsung-phone-price-in-nepal
<h2>The middle of the range, with specifications</h2>
[[models:samsung-galaxy-a07,samsung-galaxy-m36-5g,samsung-galaxy-a17-5g,samsung-galaxy-a27-5g,samsung-galaxy-a37-5g,samsung-galaxy-s26,samsung-galaxy-s25-ultra]]
<h2>Understanding the letters</h2>
<p>Samsung's naming is tidy once someone explains it. The letter is the family. A is for everyone, M and F are versions of A sold mainly online, S is the flagship line, Z folds. The first digit is the rank inside the family, from 0 to 5 in the A series. The second digit is the year. So the A07 is the basic model of the 7 generation and the A57 its best. A phone called A08 is newer than an A07 and not better than an A17.</p>
<p>"FE" means a simplified flagship. "Plus" means a larger one. "Ultra" means everything, with a pen.</p>
<h2>Why Samsung costs more for the same hardware</h2>
<p>Set a Galaxy A17 5G beside a Redmi Note 15 5G at nearly the same price, and the Redmi wins most rows: brighter screen, faster chip, larger battery, better zoom. Samsung asks you to pay for other things.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Service.</strong> Centres in most cities, with parts in stock.</li>
<li><strong>Updates.</strong> Six years on recent A models, seven on S and Z.</li>
<li><strong>Security.</strong> Knox, Secure Folder, Auto Blocker.</li>
<li><strong>Resale.</strong> A used Galaxy sells quickly at a better price.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you change phones every eighteen months, these are worth little. If you keep one for five years, they are worth more than a faster chip.</p>
<h2>Up to Rs 35,000: A07, A07s, A08</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a07">A07</a> is the one to know. It is a plain, dependable phone, and <a href="/reviews/samsung-galaxy-a07-review-nepal">our review</a> explains its limits. The A07s and A08 cost more for small changes, and at over Rs 34,000 you should look at other brands unless support is the reason. Take 6GB if you can.</p>
<h2>Rs 40,000 to 57,000: M36, M17, A17, A27</h2>
<p>This is where Samsung's own range competes with itself. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-m36-5g">M36 5G</a> at Rs 41,999 has a faster chip, a 120Hz screen and 8GB, and is the best value Samsung sells in Nepal at present. It is found online more than in shops. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a17-5g">A17 5G</a> is everywhere and a little weaker. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-a27-5g">A27 5G</a> has a Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 and 120Hz, and at Rs 55,999 it meets the Honor X9d and others with far larger batteries.</p>
<h2>Rs 75,000 to 1,08,000: A37 and A57</h2>
<p>After this year's increases these are hard to recommend at list price. The A37 in its base version makes sense for people who want IP68 and six years of support. The A57 is thin and pleasant, with a price that now touches phones with flagship chips. Both become reasonable with an exchange bonus. See our <a href="/reviews/samsung-galaxy-a37-5g-review-nepal">A37</a> and <a href="/reviews/samsung-galaxy-a57-5g-review-nepal">A57</a> reviews.</p>
<h2>Rs 1,25,000 to 1,60,000: S26 FE, S26, S26+</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s26">S26</a> is the one we like: small, fast and beautifully made, with a battery that asks for a midday top-up from heavy users. The FE is bigger and slower. The Plus brings a larger screen and battery for Rs 20,000 more and is quietly the most comfortable phone in the line for people who do not need a pen.</p>
<h2>Rs 1,85,000 and above: Ultra and foldables</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-s25-ultra">S25 Ultra</a> has become the sensible Ultra, as we argue in <a href="/news/samsung-galaxy-s25-ultra-leak-roundup-snapdragon-8-elite-nepal">this comparison</a>. The <a href="/reviews/samsung-galaxy-s26-ultra-review-nepal">S26 Ultra</a> is the complete one. Among foldables, the Flip models are stylish and compact, and the Fold models are small tablets. The <a href="/products/samsung-galaxy-z-flip7-fe">Z Flip7 FE</a> at Rs 1,35,999 is the cheapest way to own a folding phone from a brand with service here. Ask about the price of an inner screen before you fall in love.</p>
<h2>When Samsung prices move</h2>
<p>S phones launch in the first quarter and fold in summer. The months before a launch are for bargains on the outgoing model, and the pre-order window is for exchange bonuses on the new one. A phones change in spring. Dashain and Tihar bring cashback. Between these, the price in the shop is often a few thousand below the list, so ask.</p>
<h2>Exynos or Snapdragon?</h2>
<p>Some Galaxy S models use Samsung's own chip in our region and Qualcomm's elsewhere. In ordinary use they are equal. In long games the Snapdragon has usually been cooler. Do not buy an imported unit for this reason alone. You would lose the local warranty for a difference you may never feel.</p>
<h2>Checking an official Galaxy</h2>
<ol>
<li>Dial *#06# and check the IMEI in MDMS.</li>
<li>Open Samsung Members and read the warranty start.</li>
<li>Look for the distributor's sticker on the box.</li>
<li>Check the model code and region in Settings, Software information.</li>
<li>Make sure the Knox warranty bit has not been tripped on a used phone.</li>
</ol>
<p>The complete routine is in <a href="/guides/how-to-check-phone-is-official-in-nepal">how to check that a phone is official</a>.</p>
<h2>What is not in the box</h2>
<p>No charger, on any model. The right one depends on the phone: 25W for the A0 and A1 series and the small S, 45W for the A37, A57 and Plus, and 60W for the S26 Ultra. Buy genuine or a reputable PD charger with PPS. A cheap one charges slowly and warms the phone.</p>
<h2>Three settings for every new Galaxy</h2>
<ul>
<li>Battery protection at 80 per cent for nights at home.</li>
<li>Auto Blocker on, to stop unknown apps.</li>
<li>Motion smoothness at its highest, as some models ship with it lowered.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Samsung or something else?</h2>
<p>Choose Samsung for a parent, an employee, a long-term purchase or a town with a Samsung centre. Choose Xiaomi, Realme or Infinix for hardware, OnePlus for smoothness, Honor for battery, vivo for portraits, Apple for video. There is no shame in either answer. If you are still between two, use <a href="/compare">Compare</a>, then read <a href="/guides/best-phones-in-nepal-by-budget">phones by budget</a>.</p>

=== xiaomi-phone-price-in-nepal
<h2>From Redmi A to Ultra</h2>
[[models:redmi-a7-pro,redmi-15-4g,redmi-note-15-5g,poco-x7-pro,redmi-note-17-pro-max-5g,poco-f7,xiaomi-17t,xiaomi-17-ultra]]
<h2>Three names, one company</h2>
<p>Xiaomi sells phones under three brands in Nepal, and the differences are smaller than the logos suggest. <strong>Redmi</strong> is for the budget and middle, sold everywhere. <strong>POCO</strong> is for speed, sold with less advertising at a lower price. <strong>Xiaomi</strong> is for cameras and design. All run HyperOS, all are serviced by the same centres, and some phones appear under two names with different backs. When a Redmi and a POCO share a chip and screen, buy the cheaper one.</p>
<h2>The cheapest: Redmi A7 and A7 Pro</h2>
<p>Around Rs 20,000 to 22,000, for calls, chat and video. They are fine as a first or second phone. The Pro has a bigger battery and 4GB. Expect slow charging and basic cameras. Read <a href="/reviews/redmi-a7-pro-review-nepal">our A7 Pro review</a> before buying one as a main phone.</p>
<h2>The popular middle: Redmi 15, 17 and the Notes</h2>
<p>The numbers here run in parallel and confuse everyone. The plain Redmi (15, 17) is a large LCD phone with a large battery. The Redmi Note adds AMOLED, better cameras and more polish. Within the Notes there are 4G and 5G versions, Pro, Pro Plus and Pro Max, and a newer number is not always better than an older one with a higher rank.</p>
<p>Our advice, after reading every sheet:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Redmi 15 4G</strong> for battery on a small budget.</li>
<li><strong>Redmi 17 5G</strong> for battery with 5G, in the 6GB version.</li>
<li><strong>Redmi Note 15 5G</strong> for most people. It is the best all-round Redmi.</li>
<li><strong>Redmi Note 17 5G</strong> only if you want its enormous battery and do not play.</li>
<li><strong>Redmi Note 17 Pro Max</strong> for 10,000mAh and fast charging.</li>
</ul>
<p>Reviews of the <a href="/reviews/redmi-17-5g-review-nepal">17 5G</a>, <a href="/reviews/redmi-note-17-5g-review-nepal">Note 17 5G</a> and <a href="/reviews/redmi-note-17-pro-max-review-nepal">Note 17 Pro Max</a> go into detail.</p>
<h2>The fast ones: POCO</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/poco-x7-pro">X7 Pro</a> at around Rs 60,000, the <a href="/products/poco-f7">F7</a> and <a href="/products/poco-x8-pro">X8 Pro</a> at Rs 70,000 and the <a href="/products/poco-f8-pro">F8 Pro</a> at Rs 1,05,000 are, rupee for rupee, the quickest phones in Nepal. Cameras are decent, batteries large, charging fast. If you play games, start and probably finish here.</p>
<h2>The Leica ones: 15T, 17T, 17 Ultra</h2>
<p>The T models are the thoughtful choice. They give Leica-tuned cameras with a telephoto, solid chips and good screens for less than the flagships. The <a href="/products/xiaomi-15t">15T</a> at about Rs 65,000 is particularly good value as an older model. The <a href="/products/xiaomi-17-ultra">17 Ultra</a> is for photographers, with a sensor larger than most compact cameras had. <a href="/reviews/xiaomi-17-ultra-review-nepal">Our review</a> says who should carry it.</p>
<h2>HyperOS: what to expect, what to switch off</h2>
<p>It is fast and full of options. It also comes with extra apps, notifications that promote things and, on cheaper models, advertisements in system apps. Give it ten minutes on the first day:</p>
<ol>
<li>Uninstall games and shopping apps.</li>
<li>In Settings, search for "personalised" and turn off each recommendation.</li>
<li>Open the Security, Themes and File Manager apps, and turn off recommendations in their settings.</li>
<li>Revoke authorisation of the MSA system ads service.</li>
<li>Allow WhatsApp and Messenger to autostart and run without battery limits, so messages are not late.</li>
</ol>
<p>After that it is a pleasant system.</p>
<h2>Updates</h2>
<p>Xiaomi has improved. Flagships and the better Notes and POCOs now get around four Android versions and up to six years of patches. Entry models get less. Ask for the figure for your model. Updates arrive region by region, and an official global unit is the one that gets them in time.</p>
<h2>China ROM and Indian units</h2>
<p>Because Redmi is so popular, grey stock is everywhere. Phones with Chinese software may lack Nepali language support, delay notifications and be relocked or blocked by updates if the seller flashed a global version. Indian units work, and are covered by warranty in India. In Settings, About phone, the version should show Global, and the box should have a Nepali distributor's sticker. Check MDMS either way.</p>
<h2>Service and spare parts</h2>
<p>Xiaomi's service network is among the widest after Samsung. Screens for popular Notes are available even in independent shops, at moderate prices. For the Ultra and T models, parts may need ordering.</p>
<h2>Price behaviour</h2>
<p>Redmi Notes hold their price for months and then drop Rs 2,000 to 5,000 when a successor is near. POCOs fall faster. Xiaomi runs brand days, festival offers and bundle deals with buds and bands. The first month after launch often has the best bundles, and the sixth month the best cash price.</p>
<h2>Chargers in the box</h2>
<p>One of Xiaomi's better habits in Nepal is that many phones still include the fast charger. Check the box. If it is missing from a POCO that supports 90W, the seller may have removed it.</p>
<h2>Which Xiaomi phone for which person</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Grandparent:</strong> Redmi A7 Pro.</li>
<li><strong>Driver or field worker:</strong> Redmi 17 5G.</li>
<li><strong>Student:</strong> Redmi Note 15 5G, or POCO X7 Pro for games.</li>
<li><strong>Traveller:</strong> Redmi Note 17 Pro Max.</li>
<li><strong>Creator on a budget:</strong> Xiaomi 15T.</li>
<li><strong>Photographer:</strong> Xiaomi 17 Ultra.</li>
</ul>
<h2>When not to buy Xiaomi</h2>
<p>If you dislike tinkering with settings, want seven years of updates, or live where only Samsung has a centre, pay a little more for a Galaxy. If resale matters a great deal, an iPhone or Samsung holds more. If you want the cleanest software, look at OnePlus, Nothing or Motorola.</p>
<h2>What is coming</h2>
<p>The <a href="/news/xiaomi-18-pro-snapdragon-8-elite-gen-6-launch">Xiaomi 18 Pro</a> has been launched abroad, and a lightly changed <a href="/news/redmi-note-18-5g-bigger-battery-rumour">Redmi Note 18 5G</a> is rumoured. Neither is a reason to delay a purchase you need. They are reasons to bargain on the present models.</p>
<h2>Last word</h2>
<p>Xiaomi's range looks like a maze because it is one. Two numbers get you through it: the chip and the memory. Choose a Snapdragon 6, 7s or Dimensity 7000 series or better, and 8GB, and nearly any Redmi or POCO will serve you for three or four years.</p>

<h2>Six series in one brand</h2>
<p>Realme names too many phones. <h2>What Realme does well</h2>
<p>Battery and charging. No brand under Rs 50,000 is as generous with both. 45W charging appears at Rs 25,000, and batteries of 7,000 to 8,000mAh are ordinary. What it does less well is restraint: too many models, a crowded app list and modest update promises on cheap phones.</p>
<h2>Under Rs 25,000: Note 60x, Note 70, Note 80</h2>
<p>All three are for light use with the same family of basic chips. The <a href="/products/realme-note-70">Note 70</a> has the best balance with 6,300mAh. The <a href="/products/realme-note-80">Note 80</a> costs about Rs 5,000 more for small changes, as our <a href="/reviews/realme-note-80-review-nepal">review</a> points out. Choose 128GB versions.</p>
<h2>Rs 25,000 to 36,000: C71, C100i, P4 Lite, P4x, C100x</h2>

The <a href="/products/realme-c71">C71</a> is the sensible one for its charging.</p>
<h2>Rs 37,000 to 50,000: where Realme shines</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/realme-c85-pro">C85 Pro</a> is an AMOLED phone with extreme water ratings. The <a href="/products/realme-c85-5g">C85 5G</a> adds 5G and 144Hz with an LCD. Then comes the pair that makes Realme worth visiting: the <a href="/products/realme-p3">P3</a> at Rs 44,999 and the <a href="/products/realme-14-5g">14 5G</a> at Rs 49,999. <p>Be careful with the P4 4G at Rs 45,999. It shares a number with better phones and has an older 4G chip.</p>

=== honor-phone-price-in-nepal
<h2>Key models compared</h2>
[[models:honor-x6d,honor-x7d,honor-x8d,honor-x9d,honor-400,honor-600,honor-600-pro,honor-magic-8-pro]]
<h2>A brand that came back</h2>
<p>Honor began as Huawei's younger line and became independent in 2020. That matters for one reason: its phones have Google services, unlike Huawei's. In Nepal it has grown quickly by putting big batteries in slim, tough bodies and offering large storage. The ladder runs from about Rs 16,500 to the foldable Magic V5 at Rs 2,66,999.</p>
<h2>The series</h2>
<p><strong>Choice and Play:</strong> very basic. <strong>X5 and X6:</strong> entry phones. <strong>X7, X8, X9:</strong> the middle, where Honor is strongest. <strong>Number series (400, 600):</strong> cameras and style. <strong>Magic:</strong> flagships. The letter after the X number is the generation, so X9d is newer than X9c.</p>
<h2>Under Rs 30,000: choose carefully</h2>
<p>The X5 and X6 phones use the Helio G81, a basic chip, with HD screens and 15W charging in most. They are pleasant to look at and slow to use. Storage is generous in some versions, which helps. The <a href="/products/honor-x6d">X6d</a> has a better chip, and <a href="/reviews/honor-x6d-review-nepal">our review</a> covers it. <h2>X7d and X7e</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/honor-x7d">X7d</a> at Rs 33,999 comes with a 108MP camera, 8GB and 256GB or more. For people who store everything, it is excellent. The chip is an older 4G one and the screen an LCD. The X7e at Rs 39,999 has 7,500mAh, and a slower chip than the X7d, so look twice.</p>
<h2>X8d: the one for reading and video</h2>
<p>An AMOLED that reaches high brightness, a 7,000mAh battery and a slim body at Rs 39,999. No 5G, modest speed. We like it a lot for ordinary people, as <a href="/reviews/honor-x8d-review-nepal">our review</a> says.</p>
<h2>X9d: the best Honor for most</h2>
<p>For Rs 54,999 you get 8,300mAh, 66W charging, a very bright screen, a capable chip and drop protection that competitors do not attempt. It is the phone to give someone who works outdoors, travels or has small children. <a href="/reviews/honor-x9d-review-nepal">Read our review</a>.</p>

<h2>Batteries: what Honor learned first</h2>

=== apple-phone-price-in-nepal
<h2>The present range</h2>

<h2>Which iPhone is for whom?</h2>
<p>Apple's range looks long, and for most people it contains two phones. The <strong>iPhone 17</strong> is for almost everyone: a 120Hz screen, two good cameras, long battery, years of support. The <strong>18 Pro</strong> or <strong>Pro Max</strong> is for those who film and photograph seriously. The others serve a particular wish: lowest price (17e), thinness (Air), novelty (Duo) or a saving on an older flagship (17 Pro and Pro Max).</p>
<h2>Cheapest sensible iPhone</h2>
<p>The <a href="/products/iphone-17e">17e</a> at Rs 1,32,499 has a new chip, 8GB of memory and a strong battery with one rear camera. It is better than a new iPhone 15 at Rs 1,27,499, which is older by two generations, has 6GB, and does not support Apple Intelligence. The difference of Rs 5,000 decides itself. The 16 at Rs 1,46,599 sits in between and is hard to justify when the 17 is Rs 18,000 more.</p>
<h2>iPhone 17: the one we recommend</h2>
<p>For the first time the regular iPhone has the smooth display that used to be reserved for the Pro. It starts with 256GB. The front camera lets you take wide group selfies in portrait. <a href="/reviews/iphone-17-review-nepal">Our review</a> finds little to complain about except slow wired charging compared with Android and the missing telephoto.</p>
<h2>Pro models: 17 or 18?</h2>
<p>The 18 Pro and Pro Max are faster, more efficient and better at zoom. The 17 Pro Max is about Rs 37,000 cheaper and remains superb. If you keep phones for five years or more, the newer one gives you an extra year. If not, look for a sealed 17 Pro Max. The full prices by storage are in <a href="/news/iphone-18-pro-pro-max-iphone-duo-nepal-price">this article</a>.</p>
<h2>Why iPhones cost what they do here</h2>
<p>The American price excludes tax. Then come freight, duty, VAT and the reseller's margin. A phone at $799 ends up near Rs 1,65,000. It is why cousins abroad are so popular in September. Bringing one for personal use is possible within customs rules, and it must be registered in MDMS. Consider the warranty and SIM type before asking for the favour.</p>
<h2>Regions and their quirks</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>United States:</strong> eSIM only. Some carriers sell locked phones.</li>
<li><strong>China and Hong Kong:</strong> two physical SIMs. Some features differ.</li>
<li><strong>Japan and Korea:</strong> camera shutter sound.</li>
<li><strong>India and Dubai:</strong> close to the version sold here.</li>
</ul>
<p>The letters at the end of the model number in Settings, General, About tell you the region. An unlocked phone shows "No SIM restrictions" on the same page.</p>
<h2>Official sellers</h2>
<p>Apple has authorised resellers and service providers in Nepal. They give a VAT bill, a genuine unit and a route for repairs. Their prices are similar, so compare bank offers, exchange value and waiting time. Elsewhere you can find lower prices on units with a shop warranty. Decide knowingly.</p>
<h2>How to check an iPhone before paying</h2>
<ol>
<li>Enter the serial number at checkcoverage.apple.com. Look at the model and the coverage date.</li>
<li>Dial *#06# and check the IMEI in MDMS.</li>
<li>In General, About, look for "Unknown Part" notices.</li>
<li>On 15 and newer, read the battery cycle count and first use.</li>
<li>Test Face ID, True Tone, all cameras, both speakers and charging.</li>
<li>Make sure that no Apple account is signed in.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Storage: how much?</h2>
<p>128GB, on older models, is tight. 256GB is right for most. Videographers should take 512GB or record to an external SSD over USB-C on Pro models. iCloud at 50GB or 200GB costs little and protects photos.</p>
<h2>Battery and charging</h2>
<p>Battery life is good on the 17, better on the Pro Max, average on the Air. Use a USB-C PD charger of 20W or more, and 40W or above for the newer Pros. The 80 per cent limit in Settings slows ageing. After three years a genuine battery restores an iPhone better than any new feature would.</p>
<h2>Buying used in Nepal</h2>
<p>Used iPhones are a large market, with large risks. Prices depend on battery health, storage, SIM type and whether the phone has been opened. A 15 Pro in good condition is a lovely purchase. Avoid phones with non-genuine screens, missing Face ID or any account lock. If the seller says "it only needs an update", leave. Ask for the bill and a copy of identification.</p>
<h2>Repairs</h2>
<p>Screen and back glass repairs are expensive, and Face ID is tied to the board. Third-party screens reduce brightness and may trigger warnings. Use a case and a glass, and turn on Stolen Device Protection.</p>
<h2>EMI</h2>
<p>At zero interest over twelve months, an iPhone 17 is about Rs 13,750 a month. Add the bank's fee. If the cash price is lower than the EMI price, the difference is the true interest. Longer plans cost more in total. Our product pages show the monthly arithmetic.</p>
<h2>iPhone or Android?</h2>
<p>Choose iPhone for video, app quality, privacy controls, resale, and family on FaceTime and iMessage. Choose Android for battery, fast charging, zoom, price and freedom. Both are secure when updated. At Rs 1.6 lakh, Android offers more hardware, and the iPhone will be worth more in three years.</p>
